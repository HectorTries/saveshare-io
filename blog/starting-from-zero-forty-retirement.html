Starting From Zero at 40: 6 Creators on Building Retirement

Dahlia says 25 years is enough if you start now, while five more creators argue about pensions, index funds and catching up fast.

By SaveShare.io · 14 November 2026

Share this Facebook X WhatsApp Pinterest

Six creators on starting retirement saving at 40 with nothing, from a £470,000 catch up plan to a $7,000 Roth IRA

Back to Blog Pension Retirement UK US

The scene is a blank balance sheet at forty. Dahlia, the London creator behind Get On Top Money, imagines arriving at middle age with literally nothing saved and asks which five moves would matter most starting today. Her answer: open a SIPP, pay in £500 a month, opt back into the workplace pension, hold three to six months of expenses in cash, and feed a stocks and shares ISA until 65. The September clip drew 2,792 plays with 206 likes, modest numbers for a message aimed at everyone who fears they left it too late.

The tension is the calendar. Twenty five years of compounding can still build a serious pot, yet every month of delay donates growth to nobody. Waiting feels comfortable because the sums look kinder from a distance, while starting feels punishing because the first contributions seem too small to matter. Three British and three American creators take the same late start and pull it in different directions.

Dahlia

Dahlia@getontopwithdahlia

From age 40 to 65, at an average return of 7% a year, you're going to have accumulated £470,000. So you do have time if you start now. And lastly, please, please don't be put off if you don't have the amounts that I have stated here available to save, invest or contribute.

Watch on TikTok (2,792 views, 206 likes). Five moves for a 40 year old starting from scratch.

Dahlia's plan is built for the British tax system. The £500 monthly SIPP payment attracts tax relief at source, lifting it to £625 equivalent, and the workplace pension adds employer money on top, a minimum of 3 percent that no market can replicate. Late starters should push contributions to the highest affordable percentage, because the shorter runway demands heavier funding, and build the emergency fund alongside, not first.

"I would opt back in immediately and make sure that I'm contributing enough to get the employer match. Because employer contributions are essentially 100% worth of free money and there is no market return that is gonna match that."

Dahlia on TikTok

If Dahlia is the strategist, Steve is the drill sergeant. A millionaire former teacher posting as Call to Leap, he gives the 40s and 50s crowd five steps for 2026, beginning not with a pension form but with a clearout: sell a fifth of everything you own, starting with the closet and the garage. The video drew 5,771 plays with 282 likes and 109 comments.

Steve

Steve@calltoleap

Sell 20% of your belongings that you don't use and don't bring you joy. Start with your closet and garage and sell them on offer up and Facebook Marketplace. If you use the Marie Kondo strategy, you'll be extremely grateful.

Watch on TikTok (5,771 views, 282 likes, 109 comments). Five steps for the 40s and 50s crowd.

His middle steps slow the pace: save $100 a month, rising by $25 monthly for a year, and read the canon, from The Simple Path to Wealth to The Millionaire Next Door. Then the tempo snaps up with aggressive income building, whatever converts spare evenings into contributions, before the money finally meets low cost index funds at step five.

"Aggressively increase your income to make up for lost time. I like dog sitting on Rover, tutoring on Wise Ant, or using Upwork for any freelance work."

Steve on TikTok

Tatiana brings the receipts. With over £140,000 in her pension, she films Pensions 101 for women falling behind, and her explainer drew 1,160 plays. Her case rests on four pillars: the state pension covers basics at best, employer contributions are free money, the tax treatment is the most generous available, and the long lockup lets compounding work.

Tatiana

Tatiana@tatianamamixo

As someone who has over £140,000 invested in my pension and has been doubling down on it this year as an investment vehicle of choice, let's get into the basics of what a pension is and why it is so important that you should have one. A pension is a long term, tax efficient savings pot built up over your working life to fund your retirement.

Watch on TikTok (1,160 views). Why a pension is essential, in four reasons.

Her second video answers the job hopper's dread: where actually is the money. Four employers across thirteen years had scattered her savings until she consolidated everything into one pot, cutting logins and hidden fees while gaining control over investments. The clip drew only 480 plays, but late starters cannot afford old pots languishing in expensive default funds.

"I've had four employers in my 13 year career span and I know where all of my pension investments are. Do you? The reason for this is that I have consolidated my pensions into one pot, and this has three main benefits to it."

Tatiana on TikTok

Nobody here sells starting small harder than Trell. The trainer turned finance creator calculated his portfolio, built from almost nothing six or seven years ago, would reach $11 million by retirement even if he never added another penny. His telling exploded: 435,976 plays, 63,240 likes, 747 comments and 15,399 shares. The engine was $7,000, moved into a Roth IRA and pointed at the market.

Trell

Trell@trellthetrainer

I just started investing six or seven years ago. I've been able to turn $7,000 into almost $400,000.

Watch on TikTok (435,976 views, 63,240 likes, 747 comments, 15,399 shares). Turning $7,000 into almost $400,000.

His prescription starts with the plumbing: move cash from checking into a high yield savings account, route the paycheque there, and run spending through a cleared credit card. From that base, $250 a month auto flows into a Roth IRA and broad index ETFs. Today's habits, he warns, choose what sixty looks like.

"Seven years ago, I saved my first $10,000. I took seven of that $10,000 and I opened a Roth IRA."

Trell on TikTok

Danny supplies the missing instruction manual. Where Trell shows the destination, the Dimes with Danny creator films the taps: open a Roth IRA in the Vanguard, Fidelity or Schwab app in minutes, contribute up to the $7,000 annual limit, then buy an S and P 500 ETF like VOO. His walkthrough drew 17,021 plays with 1,067 likes. Depositing the money is not investing it, and countless savers sit in cash for years believing they are in the market.

Danny

Danny@dimeswithdanny

So many people mess up here and think that they're done, but we are not done. All we've done is added money into the account, but now we need to actually invest it.

Watch on TikTok (17,021 views, 1,067 likes). Opening a Roth IRA on your phone.

Danny films for young beginners, yet his lesson lands harder at forty, when every uninvested month costs more. A late starter who funds the account but forgets the final buy order has built a savings account with extra steps. That a couple of minutes is all it takes reads as a rebuke to anyone who has spent years meaning to start.

"With a Roth IRA, you can only contribute $7,000 a year. So here it shows you how much you've contributed and how much you have left."

Danny on TikTok

Ciara proves the system works on British soil. She went from zero to £100,000 invested in just under five years, and her payday routine drew 33,418 plays with 2,242 likes. Her three moves mirror Dahlia's: build three to six months of expenses in an easy access account, kill debt above 7 percent, then invest through the workplace match and a stocks and shares ISA.

Ciara

Ciara@ciara__anne

Do these three things as soon as you get paid. I went from zero to £100,000 invested in just under five years. And these are the three things I wish I knew.

Watch on TikTok (33,418 views, 2,242 likes). Zero to £100,000 invested in under five years.

Meet the full employer match, because anything less leaves free money behind, then use the ISA allowance for global ETFs such as VAUG or VWRP, where gains compound free of tax. She is candid that emergency funds take years. The tone is automation over agony: set the payday transfers and let routine do what willpower cannot.

"If your employer offers a workplace pension match, you should be meeting That so if they say we'll invest 5%, if you invest 5%, you bet you should be investing 5%. Otherwise you're leaving free money on the table."

Ciara on TikTok

Where they agree, and where they split

On three points the six speak with one voice. Employer matching money is free and non negotiable, called out by name on the British side while Trell and Danny build their American system around funding the Roth first. Starting now beats starting big, from Steve's $100 first month to Trell's first $10,000. And the vehicle is always the low cost index fund, whether VWRP, VOO or simply the tracker. The weight of voices sits firmly here, and nobody dissents.

"Because employer contributions are essentially 100% worth of free money and there is no market return that is gonna match that."

Dahlia on TikTok

The splits are about wrappers and temperament. Dahlia and Tatiana argue pension first, since British tax relief and employer matching multiply each pound, while Trell and Danny default to the Roth IRA and its tax free growth. Steve's path is the most physical: sell belongings, hustle for income, treat lost time as a debt. Ciara answers with calm automation, trusting payday transfers over side gigs. Both camps agree the late starter must contribute more; they differ only on whether the extra comes from earning more or automating better.

"Seven years ago, I saved my first $10,000. I took seven of that $10,000 and I opened a Roth IRA."

Trell on TikTok

Open the pension this week, opting back into the workplace scheme, starting a SIPP, or funding a Roth, and claim every pound of employer match. Automate one monthly investment into a broad index fund, sweep old pensions and idle cash into the plan, clear debt above 7 percent, and keep three to six months of expenses in reach. Twenty five years is enough, but only if the first contribution leaves this month.
