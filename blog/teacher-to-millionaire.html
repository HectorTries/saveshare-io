Teacher to Millionaire: The Slow Path Elly and Mia Walk

How educators build serious wealth on ordinary salaries

← All money advice Wealth building

You don't need a finance salary to build wealth. Two UK creators — a primary teacher and a former fashion employee — show how ordinary incomes compound into extraordinary outcomes.

Elly: primary teacher on the millionaire track

Elly (@msellyphant, 36.5K followers) describes herself as a primary teacher by day — "on track to retire a MILLIONAIRE." Her content breaks down how a teacher's salary, invested consistently over a full career plus pension, can compound into seven figures.

Mia: from fashion 9-to-5 to £200K+

Mia Rose (@miarosemcgrath) worked in fashion, felt one income wasn't secure enough, and built content creation as a second stream. Her advice to anyone living at home and working: save a three-month emergency fund first, then save the equivalent of your region's average rent every month to psychologically prepare for independence.

— Mia Rose (TikTok source ↗)

The side-hustle accelerator

Mia again: "Having a side hustle really helped me. I don't ever want to be reliant on one source of income." For teachers and other salaried workers, a second income stream aimed straight at investments compresses the timeline dramatically.

— Mia Rose (TikTok source ↗)

Systems over salary

Hannah Bevington (@hannahbevington): "Building wealth doesn't just happen. You really have to curate it and put certain systems in place." Automate investing on payday; salary size matters less than the percentage you keep.

— Hannah Bevington (TikTok source ↗)

Why it works

Teachers have three hidden advantages: inflation-linked pensions, long holidays for side projects, and iron job security that lets them invest aggressively through downturns. Combine a 30-year horizon with index funds and the maths does the heavy lifting.

The one rule

Invest a fixed percentage on payday, increase it with every pay rise, and let time do the rest.
