Don't Fear Market Dips: Lupes on ETFs and Buying the Fall

Why falling markets are a gift to monthly investors

Lupes (Lupes) opens with a plea every long-term investor needs to hear:

When markets fall, your regular monthly investment buys more shares for the same money, pound-cost averaging working exactly as designed. Lupes' message cuts against every instinct: the red days are when long-term wealth is built, because units bought cheap compound for decades. Sellers lock in losses; holders collect the recovery.

One low-cost global ETF gives you thousands of companies in a single purchase, instant diversification with no stock-picking required.

DiDi Finance (DiDi) asks the question beginners are afraid to ask: Her answer: global trackers, ethical funds and different index flavours all exist, the key is picking a diversified fund and sticking with it, not copying someone else's exact ticker.

Lupes again: Janice (Janice) goes further: Time in the market beats timing the market, and small starts build the habit that carries big money later.

Open a Stocks & Shares ISA with a low-cost platform: Vanguard, AJ Bell, Fidelity and Trading 212 all offer global index funds with fees under 0.25% a year. Set up a monthly direct debit, pick one fund (a FTSE Global All Cap or MSCI World tracker covers thousands of companies), and switch off. The whole setup takes under an hour, and the automation means you keep buying through every dip without lifting a finger. Ollie Invests (Ollie Invests) puts it bluntly: That is the entire strategy: one fund, every month, for decades. Fees are the silent killer, a 1% annual fee can devour nearly a third of your returns over 40 years, so cheap trackers beat expensive active funds before you even start. And inside an ISA wrapper, every penny of growth is shielded from UK tax, which is why creators call the ISA the single most important account a young Brit can open.

The 2008 financial crisis, the 2020 Covid crash, the 2022 inflation slump, every single one looked like the end of the world and every single one recovered to new highs within a few years. Investors who kept buying through 2008 tripled their money within a decade. The data behind Lupes' plea is a century of stock market history: diversified global equities have never failed to recover given enough time, and regular buyers during crashes end up with the best lifetime returns of all.

Automate a monthly ETF buy, ignore the dips, better yet, welcome them, and never wait until you feel "ready".
