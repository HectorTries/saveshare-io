Women and Money: Amy, Kafilat, DiDi and the Creators Closing the Gap

Systems, investing and confidence, advice from the women of UK money TikTok

← All money advice Women & money

The statistics are stark and familiar: British women hold less in investments than men, retire with smaller pensions, and report lower confidence about money at every income level. Against that backdrop, a generation of UK creators has decided the investing gap is a content problem, that women do not need simpler advice, they need advice that speaks to them, from people who look like them. Their videos are closing the gap one portfolio at a time.

Amy of Financially Her states her mission as an invitation: content "for women that want more, from their career, money & life." The framing matters. Where traditional finance speaks in warnings and jargon, Amy speaks in ambition, money confidence begins with treating your finances as a project worthy of the same energy as your career. Her 45,600 followers suggest the message was overdue.

Kafilat aims at the specific failure mode of high-earning women: income without infrastructure. She helps "millennial women earning well build systems" for their money, because a good salary with no automation still ends most months at zero. Her prescription runs automation first, guilt never: direct debits before discipline.

DiDi makes the whole thing communal. "Let's become financially free together," she tells 18,200 followers, investing reframed from solitary homework to shared journey. The community wrapper matters more than it sounds; research consistently finds women prefer learning about money socially, and DiDi's comment sections function as study groups.

Then the practitioners take the floor. Sophie Blank, sitting on £230,000, delivers the line that launched a thousand portfolios: "Rich girls don't panic and pick stocks, they buy the market and move on. Every month, I buy exactly 3 funds."

Via Sophie Blank (TikTok source ↗)

Hannah Bevington, £135,000 at 26, states the philosophy behind the mechanics: "Building wealth doesn't just happen. You really have to curate it and put certain systems in place."

Via Hannah Bevington (TikTok source ↗)

Sophie closes the loop with the unglamorous engine room of it all: "My sinking funds are the one reason I pay off my credit card every single month and I never go into debt." Pots for predictable spending, the unsexy infrastructure beneath every confident portfolio.

Via Sophie Blank (TikTok source ↗)

Open the ISA, automate the monthly purchase, talk about money openly. Visibility compounds too.
