The $100K Ceiling: 4 Mistakes Keeping Your Net Worth Down

Humphrey names the four leaks, Mia builds the ladder, Danny shows the apps, and Georgia defends the first £500.

By SaveShare.io · 16 November 2026

Share this Facebook X WhatsApp Pinterest

Four leaks that cap your net worth below six figures, and the ladder three creators built to climb past it

Back to Blog Net Worth US UK

The first $100,000 is famously the hardest, and Humphrey Yang thinks he knows exactly why most people never get there. In a September video that drew 29,800 plays with 1,255 likes and 96 comments, he lists four money mistakes that keep people below $100K in net worth, and warns the last one is the most common of all. The framing is blunt: these are not bad luck or low wages, they are leaks, and every one of them is fixable.

The tension underneath is uncomfortable. Everyone agrees compounding does the heavy lifting after six figures, yet the behaviour that gets you there looks boring: drive a cheaper car, skip the bets, know your savings rate, kill the card balance. Nobody wants the answer to be discipline plus arithmetic. Three more creators take Humphrey's diagnosis and build the treatment plan, from milestones to apps to the psychology of celebrating small wins.

Humphrey Yang

Humphrey Yang@humphreytalks

Four money mistakes that keep you below 100K in net worth. And the last one is actually the most common. Number one is a new car with a huge payment. The average new car payment is now 770 dollars per month. And then if you add in insurance, gas, and maintenance, you're closer to 1000 to 1200 dollars per month. If you make 65K a year, that's about 25% of your take home pay.

Watch on TikTok (29,800 views, 1,255 likes, 96 comments). Four mistakes keeping you below $100K.

Mistake two is prediction markets and sports gambling, which Humphrey calls speculative shortcuts that 99 percent of people lose at long term. Mistake three is not knowing your savings rate, the hidden leak that makes you think you save 15 percent when you really save 5. And mistake four, the most common, is carrying a credit card balance at over 21 percent interest, which means losing money faster than Warren Buffett ever made it. His fix for the car is simple: drive something slightly older with a reasonable payment.

"Carrying a balance means that you're losing money at a rate greater than what the GOAT of investing could make per year."

Humphrey Yang on TikTok

Humphrey returned to the theme in a follow up on what becoming a millionaire actually feels like, and the answer doubles as motivation for the climb. Nothing happens at $1,000,000, he says, just a digit ticking over on a screen, and a Harvard study found people at almost every wealth level want two to three times more to feel happy. The goalposts always move. His point is not that the milestone is hollow but that the habits matter more than the number, because the number never satisfies on its own. A 3 percent down day on a million dollar portfolio is $30,000 of paper losses, which takes emotional conditioning most people never build. Starting the discipline at $500, when the swings are painless, is what prepares you for the swings at $500,000. The ceiling is training for what comes after it.

"Think of your net worth like a stock price. It'll go up, but you won't really feel like a millionaire unless you really change how you live."

Humphrey Yang on TikTok

If Humphrey is the diagnostician, Mia Rose is the architect. At 25, with what she describes as a multiple six figure net worth, she filmed four financial milestones to hit in your 20s, and it drew 71,024 plays with 5,501 likes. Her ladder: three months of expenses saved, high interest debt eliminated, a stocks and shares ISA opened, and a 20 percent savings rate reached.

Mia Rose

Mia Rose@miarosemcgrath

These are four financial milestones I think you should be trying to hit in your 20s. This is coming from a 25 year old that has a multiple six figure net worth. And I spent my early 20s increasing my income and reducing my expenses, all with the eventual goal of getting to financial freedom.

Watch on TikTok (71,024 views, 5,501 likes, 219 shares). Four milestones for your 20s.

Her debt advice maps directly onto Humphrey's fourth mistake: list every balance with its rate, then kill consumer debt with the avalanche method for maths or the snowball for momentum. On investing she keeps it deliberately boring, broad market index funds in the ISA, starting with £50 or £100, because the longer you are invested, the lower your odds of losing money. And she gives the savings rate formula outright: monthly saved divided by monthly income, times 100.

"The earlier you start, the less you have to invest to get the same results as someone that started much later than you. And that's because of compound interest."

Mia Rose on TikTok

His setup answers Humphrey's third mistake directly. The Notion tracker means his savings rate is measured, not guessed, and the Experian habit means no balance surprises. It is the least glamorous video on this page and the most liked, which says something about what people actually want: not motivation, but a parts list.

Georgia brings the counterweight the others need. Posting as Money Wellness Girl, she admits upfront she has neither £50K nor £100K saved, then argues her smaller milestones deserve celebrating anyway. The video drew 19,791 plays with 1,386 likes, and its message lands hardest on anyone measuring themselves against Mia and Danny.

Georgia

Georgia@moneywellnessgirl

I don't have 50K saved, and I definitely don't have 100K saved. But that doesn't mean I'm not proud of myself for saving everything I have up until this point. Your £500 net worth might not seem that impressive next to someone else's 50K net worth, but if you had nothing saved six months ago, that £500 is massive.

Watch on TikTok (19,791 views, 1,386 likes, 35 comments). Why small milestones count.

Her point is behavioural, not mathematical. Comparison kills the savings habit before compounding can start, so she prescribes celebration: tell your friends, buy the cake, mark the £100, £250 and £500 thresholds. Humphrey would call this soft; Georgia would answer that nobody sticks with a plan that never lets them win.

"When you spend enough time on the internet, it is so easy to compare your financial timeline to other people's and feel like what you have isn't enough."

Georgia on TikTok

Where they agree, and where they split

On the mechanics there is no daylight. Humphrey, Mia and Danny all treat the savings rate as the master number: measure it honestly, raise it steadily, automate it so willpower is not load bearing. All four agree high interest debt goes first, with Mia's avalanche versus snowball choice being the only tactical debate. And all four assume boring index funds over speculation, with Humphrey explicitly warning that gambling shortcuts fail 99 percent of players.

"It hides every other mistake. So if you think you save 15%, but you really save 5%, you're three times further from 100K than you might think."

Humphrey Yang on TikTok

The split is about pressure versus patience. Humphrey's camp says feel the urgency: a $770 car payment is eating a quarter of your take home, a 21 percent card balance outruns Buffett, act now. Georgia's camp says urgency without self compassion burns people out before the first £500 becomes £5,000. Mia bridges the two, pairing hard milestones with the reminder that £50 a month in your 20s still counts.

List every debt with its rate, kill the card balance first, and know your real savings rate this week, not the one you imagine. Then set the ladder: three months of expenses, debt cleared, investing account opened, 20 percent saved. Celebrate the £500 like Georgia says, then automate past it like Mia and Danny do. The ceiling is not income. It is the four leaks, patched one by one.
