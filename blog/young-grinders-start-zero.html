He Invests £10 a Week: Mia, Jake, Trell and the Young Grinders Starting From Zero

Small deposits, cleared debts and the first $10K.

By SaveShare.io · 19 October 2026

Small deposits, cleared debts and the first $10K.

Back to Blog Saving Start investing UK

For seventy eight weeks, through every headline, Sean put £10 into his Stocks and Shares ISA. Six pounds into a global ETF, three into an S and P 500 fund, one pound into semiconductors. The total invested came to £780. The portfolio sat at £941.83. The money was almost beside the point. A year and a half of showing up every week had taught him how investing actually feels, which is something no amount of reading could have given him.

Sean

Sean@seansmoney

For the last 78 weeks, I have invested just £10 every week. I have invested £780, and the portfolio is now worth £941.83. The biggest benefit has not been the returns. It is that I have spent a year and a half learning, building consistency, and becoming comfortable with investing. Those habits will still be valuable when I can invest much larger amounts. You do not need to start big. You just need to start.

Watch on TikTok (the £10 a week video, 78 weeks of consistency).

The tension underneath his little experiment is the one that freezes most beginners. Wait until you earn more, or start now with almost nothing. Sean's answer is that waiting has a hidden price, because the habit has to be built at some point and it is cheapest to build it when the stakes are tiny. A £10 week that goes wrong costs less than a takeaway. A £1,000 month gone wrong, made by someone who never practised, costs a great deal more.

Mia Rose gives that audience a map. At 25, with a multiple six figure net worth built over seven years of saving, she recorded the four milestones she thinks everyone should chase in their twenties. Her most watched videos each drew tens of thousands of viewers, with her investing explainer passing 66,000 views and her milestones video passing 71,000.

Mia Rose

Mia Rose@miarosemcgrath

These are four financial milestones I think you should be trying to hit in your 20s. This is coming from a 25 year old that has a multiple six figure net worth. And I spent my early 20s increasing my income and reducing my expenses, all with the eventual goal of getting to financial freedom. Number one is have 3 months worth of living expenses saved. This is your emergency fund.

Watch on TikTok (71,000 views, four milestones for your 20s).

Milestone two is where her map turns strict. Clear the expensive consumer debt, credit cards and buy now pay later balances, before anything ambitious. She distinguishes the two classic payoff methods for her viewers, the avalanche that kills the highest rate first and the snowball that kills the smallest balance first for momentum, and tells them to list every debt with its rate and start working. It is the least glamorous milestone and the one she refuses to let anyone skip.

"Number two get rid of high interest debt. I am talking credit cards, Klarna. There are two main methods the debt avalanche method and the debt snowball method. The debt avalanche method aims to pay off the highest interest first so that you mathematically end up paying less. The debt snowball method is about paying off the smallest amounts first to get the ball rolling and build momentum."Mia Rose (watch on TikTok)

Then comes the part nobody explained to her, which is why she explains it to everyone else. Why invest at all, and how, without jargon, without a finance degree, without financially literate parents. Her answer starts with inflation quietly shrinking every pound left in a current account, and ends with her own position, £100,000 invested, learned from seven years of research and curiosity.

"So everyone is telling you to invest your money, but no one is ever properly sitting down and explaining to you how to actually do that step by step? I built a multiple six figure net worth in my 20s. I have 100K invested and I did not grow up financially literate. I never worked in finance, I do not have financially literate parents."Mia Rose (watch on TikTok)

Jake Shuttleworth is what happens when milestone two becomes a whole personality. A 31 year old documenting his self improvement journey, he cleared about £23,000 of debt in roughly 18 months, then made a follow up video about what the payoff taught him. That follow up drew 32,474 views with 1,513 likes, because paying debt off is one story but staying out turns out to be the harder and more useful one.

Jake Shuttleworth

Jake Shuttleworth@jake_shuttler

Here is everything I have learned since paying off over £20,000 worth of debt in a year and a half and how I plan on staying out of debt. The first thing, no impulse buying. I was quite careless with money. If I saw something I liked it, I bought it, did not care about how I was going to pay for it. I think almost everything I have bought over the past six years I do not have anymore and that is the truth.

Watch on TikTok (32,474 views, 1,513 likes, staying debt free).

His diagnosis of how he got into debt is gentler than most finance content allows. He spent his twenties feeling lost, with no financial goals, no house dream, no reason to say no to anything. The debt was a symptom of aimlessness as much as spending..

"As of last month, I officially paid off about £23,000 worth of debt in roughly 18 months. Now, I am not gonna lie to you, it was hard work. There was some grafting involved. There was some sleepless nights. There was a lot of tears however I did it. I am a perfect example that if you really set your mind to it and you plan out your actions and you really commit everything to becoming debt free it is possible."Jake Shuttleworth (watch on TikTok)

His practical fix for staying free is almost embarrassingly simple. No impulse buying, enforced by waiting. If he wants something, clothing or anything else, he sits on it for a few weeks, and most desires expire on their own. It is the behavioural mirror of Sean's automation, friction in one direction, flow in the other. Jake adds friction to spending. Sean removed friction from investing.

Trell the Trainer operates at a different volume. His savings videos reach hundreds of thousands, with one drawing over 2.6 million views and 225,443 likes, and his message is intensity itself. Save the first $10,000 because that first pile changes what the rest of your money can do. He ran the maths on his own portfolio, showing what it could be worth at retirement even if he never added another penny, and the number he landed on was eleven million.

Trell the Trainer

Trell the Trainer@trellthetrainer

Let me show you why it is so important to save your first $10,000. I just did the math on what my retirement portfolio would be worth at retirement if I just stopped investing today. If I never invested another penny into my investment portfolio. It is 11 million dollars waiting on me at retirement.

Watch on TikTok (435,976 views, 63,240 likes, the first $10K).

His most combative video lays out five rules for getting rich, and the first is the one that stops scrollers cold. Disappear for six months. No dating, no birthdays, no trips. Stack everything. He knows almost nobody will do it, and that is the point. The top one percent is not smarter, he argues, just more willing to delay gratification for half a year.

"First thing is you are gonna have to take six months off. Six months off from everything. Dating, birthdays, trips. Six months. That is all it takes. Think about how many people can not do that. Imagine if you go missing for six months and you stack up everything. You will be set for the rest of your life."Trell the Trainer (watch on TikTok)

Behind the shouting sits a genuinely instructive origin story. Trell put his first $7,000 of trainer income into the stock market and kept feeding it, around 70 percent of his income for six or seven years, until the position approached half a million dollars.

Ollie Invests brings the quiet mechanics that Trell's intensity skips over. His portfolio tour video walks beginners through the actual allocation, most of it into a global tracker, smaller slices into growth and precious metals, contributed monthly and left alone for decades. His illustration is deliberately humble, £50 a week compounding toward a possible £270,000 over a long horizon.

Ollie Invests

Ollie Invests@ollieinvests

So the common advice always to use ETFs, diversify your investments and think long term. That way there is no guesswork needed because you have diversified your risk. So as you can see, 70 percent of my money goes straight into a FTSE world fund. I just put my money into this every month and try to increase it. And I intend to invest into this for 10, 20, 30 years.

Where they agree, and where they split

On the starting question there is total agreement. Everyone here says start before you feel ready, with amounts that feel almost silly. Sean's £10 weeks, Ollie's £50 illustration, Mia's three month emergency fund as milestone one, Trell's first $10,000 as the pile that changes everything, Jake's insistence that planning beats earning. Nobody waits for a windfall. Nobody recommends timing the market. The unanimous vote is that the first deposit matters more than its size, because the habit is the actual asset.

The split opens on intensity. Trell prescribes six months of near monastic stacking, vanishing from social life to build the pile. Jake prescribes something closer to mindfulness, waiting a few weeks before each purchase and letting desires die quietly. Mia sits between them, systematic but sustainable, raising income while trimming expenses over seven years rather than six months. The awkward truth is that Trell's method builds fastest and breaks most people, while Jake's builds slowest and keeps working. Sean's automation dodges the question elegantly, because a standing order requires neither heroics nor willpower after the first setup.

The second split is debt versus investing order. Mia and Jake are strict, expensive consumer debt dies before ambitious investing begins. Sean complicates this in a useful way, because his £10 weeks ran while ordinary life continued, suggesting small parallel investing keeps the habit warm even during a payoff. Ollie would likely side with Mia on expensive debt but his decades framing implies the greater risk is years spent out of the market.

The practical close borrows one move from each voice. Open the investing account this week and automate a small weekly deposit in the style of Sean, even a tenner. List every debt with its rate in the style of Mia and attack the expensive ones first. Add Jake's waiting rule to every non essential purchase, a few weeks of patience before any buying. Name the first savings target in the style of Trell, ten thousand of your own currency, and picture what decades of compounding do to it in the style of Ollie. Readers who outgrow this page graduate naturally to the early retirement end of the spectrum, where Rebecca's invest and rest method shows what the grind compounds into. Different ages, different countries, identical method, start small, kill the expensive debt, automate the rest.
