5 Monthly Money Habits That Keep Dahlia Five Steps Ahead

Know your numbers, automate your bills and investments, plus Tatiana's budget-with-me transparency and Janice's £10K uni-portfolio system

Dahlia (@getontopwithdahlia) opens with the problem every payday brings: "Here are the five ways that I get on top of my finances every month so I'm always five steps ahead and not ending the month wondering where my money went and not having saved or invested a thing." Her answer is five habits, all systems, no willpower.

Via getontopwithdahlia (TikTok source ↗)

Habit 1: Know every outgoing to the decimal point

"I know exactly to the decimal point what is going to come out of my account every month in regards to my mortgage and bills. That also includes all my app subscriptions, gym memberships, and anything else that is a regular monthly occurrence. If you don't know what your numbers are, I recommend you take this step ASAP."

Via getontopwithdahlia (TikTok source ↗)

Habit 2: Direct-debit every bill on the 1st

"I have every single one of my bills, including debt repayments like credit cards, all direct debited every month on the first of the month, straight out of my account. That way for the rest of the month, I know exactly how much money I have left to spend."

Via getontopwithdahlia (TikTok source ↗)

Habit 3: Auto-invest 10% into one global fund

"I direct debit 10% of my monthly take home pay into my investing account automatically every month. That way I keep growing my investment pot without having to think about transferring that money across. At the moment, my primary investment allocation is in VWRP, which is the Vanguard FTSE All World, my money is automatically invested into over 3,800 global companies across every sector. But most importantly, I never have to think about where I'm investing my money and I can just get on with my life."

Via getontopwithdahlia (TikTok source ↗)

Habits 4 and 5: Beat bill creep and keep a buffer

"I have alerts set for all of my bill providers to tell me when the contract is coming to an end so I can look for a better deal." Contract-renewal alerts kill the loyalty penalty: UK regulators estimate loyal customers overpay hundreds of pounds a year on energy, broadband and insurance. Pair that with an emergency buffer and the system is complete: fixed costs known, bills automated, investing automated, renewals challenged.

Via getontopwithdahlia (TikTok source ↗)

Tatiana budgets with you, down to the pound

Tatiana (@tatianamamixo) proves habit 1 in public every month: "Come budget with me as a senior account director working in tech sales in London… my net pay was £5,961. Bear in mind, this is after all deductions, including my taxes and pension, salary sacrifice." She then walks through every fixed expense, £650 rent (living at home), £63 life insurance, £46 critical illness, £28 phone, £22 gym, £37 subscriptions. Radical line-by-line transparency is what makes Dahlia's habit 1 real rather than aspirational.

Via tatianamamixo (TikTok source ↗)

Janice automated her way to £10K at uni

Janice (@janiice.myla) is the student-budget proof of habit 3: "I left uni with £10,000 in my investment portfolio. Firstly, I would automate an amount that I would put into my Trading 212 account every month. I chose £100 cause I had a part time job at the time. But you could literally do it with any amount, £2, a fiver, a tenner. I only put my money into ETFs and index funds as historically they're known to have an 8% return every year." Same system as Dahlia, smaller numbers, same compounding engine.

Via janiice.myla (TikTok source ↗)

The one rule

Know every outgoing, pay every bill on the 1st, and auto-invest a fixed slice into one global fund, then get on with your life.
