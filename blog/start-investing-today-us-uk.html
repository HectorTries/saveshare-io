Start Investing Today: How 4 Creators Turned Small First Deposits Into Six Figures

Ten thousand dollars, twenty pounds, and the compounding that followed

By SaveShare.io · 18 October 2026

Share this Facebook X WhatsApp Pinterest

Back to Blog Investing Retirement

Trell the Trainer opens with a number and a dare. The number is eleven million dollars, his projection for his own retirement portfolio even if he never invests another penny. The dare is smaller, almost insultingly small beside it. Save the first ten thousand. In a video watched 435,976 times, he traces the path between those two figures, six or seven years of investing that turned an early deposit into nearly four hundred thousand dollars. The scene matters more than the sums. A trainer who works with young men escaping paycheck to paycheck living points at the starting line instead of the finish, and insists the distance between them is mostly time.

The tension underneath every retirement pitch is the beginner stalemate. Waiting until pay rises, knowledge deepens, or confidence arrives feels like prudence, yet compounding treats delay as the costliest choice on the menu. Small deposits made early keep beating larger deposits made late, which means the responsible feeling move and the mathematical move point in opposite directions. The four creators below attack that stalemate from four corners, an American trainer with a Roth IRA receipt, an American instructor issuing start today orders, a British saver publishing her net worth in plain figures, and a British graduate filming the twenty pound first step.

Trell the Trainer draws his credibility from recency rather than tenure. He is not recalling decisions from a different economy decades ago. He saved his first ten thousand seven years ago, placed seven thousand of it into a Roth IRA, and left compounding to work in public. The clip now carries 63,240 likes and 15,399 shares across 747 comments, which suggests the starting line message found its audience.

Trell the Trainer

Trell the Trainer@trellthetrainer

I just started investing six or seven years ago. I have been able to turn $7,000 into almost $400,000. Seven years ago, I saved my first $10,000. I took seven of that $10,000 and I opened a Roth IRA.

Watch on TikTok (435,976 views, 63,240 likes, 747 comments, 15,399 shares). His first ten thousand went to a high yield account, then seven thousand moved into a Roth IRA.

Around the headline figures sits a plain operating system. Move pay into a high yield savings account on arrival, run daily spending through a card that is cleared in full, and route 250 dollars a month into the Roth automatically. Inside the account he names broad index funds alongside a few single stocks, then pointedly stops checking. The neglect is the strategy. Money left alone in markets has time to compound, while money watched daily gets traded on nerves.

Melissa Jean-Baptiste converts the same starting line into orders. She is 38 and describes herself as on track to retire with 3.3 million dollars, and she presents the path as non negotiable rather than advisory. Her briefing, delivered in a video with 906 views and 175 likes, reads like an enlistment form for future millionaires. The small audience is worth noting. Obscurity does not make instruction wrong, and her curriculum matches the standard playbook almost line for line.

Melissa Jean-Baptiste

Melissa Jean-Baptiste@millennialindebt

I am 38 years old and I am on track to retire with $3.3 million. I am also not afraid to hurt your feelings so listen to me when I tell you you are over complicating the process. You are going to start investing and you are going to start investing today.

Watch on TikTok (906 views, 175 likes). The choice she offers is 3 percent less today or millions in retirement tomorrow.

Her signature metaphor does quiet work. An IRA is a shopping cart and investments are the groceries, which means transferring money without investing it leaves the shopper pushing an empty cart home. The fix she prescribes is deliberately anti precious, twenty five dollars into a total market index fund, done the same day. The amount is trivial and the act is everything, because the barrier is psychological and only motion dissolves it.

If your job offers a 401k you are going to sign up immediately immediately immediately. You are gonna start with a 3 percent contribution which means your checks are going to be a little bit smaller which means it is probably going to hurt a little bit.

Melissa Jean-Baptiste on TikTok

The bluntness is doing pedagogical work of its own. She frames the smaller paycheck as the price of admission and hands the viewer an explicit choice, 3 percent less today or millions tomorrow. Then she adds the maintenance rules, capture the full employer match, raise contributions with every raise, open the IRA, fund it, invest the transfer. It is the entire American retirement sequence compressed into one sitting, delivered at drill volume.

Tatiana supplies the British evidence that compounding rewards the early. She films her net worth the way others film workouts, in public and on the record. Her milestone video drew 6,858 views with 1,283 likes and 185 comments, and its power comes from one subtraction. She began the year at 282,000 pounds, saved 85,000 pounds in total, and crossed 400,000 pounds anyway. The gap between what she set aside and what she holds is the market doing its share of the labour.

Tatiana

Tatiana@tatianamamixo

I have just hit 400,000 pounds net worth. That is insane to me. At the start of this year, my net worth was only 282,000 pounds. Because I have only saved 85,000 pounds to date, and yet here we are, crossing 400,000 pounds net worth.

Watch on TikTok (6,858 views, 1,283 likes, 185 comments). She began the year at 282,000 pounds and saved 85,000 pounds toward the milestone.

Her account of the result is disarmingly ordinary. She wrote the goal in a journal at the start of the year, called it lofty, and kept her promises in public. No windfall appears in the story and no secret tactic either, just bold stated targets plus steady investing plus an audience holding her to both. She describes herself as a regular saver with no financial help, which is precisely why the figures land. If the math works for her, the viewer reasons, the viewer has run out of exemptions.

Janice answers the question the other three skip, which is what the literal first twenty pounds should do on day one. A 22 year old graduate with more than 10,000 pounds invested, she films the beginner sequence tap by tap, and the video found a large audience, 167,333 views with 16,758 likes, 451 comments and 2,241 shares. The popularity reads as demand for specificity. People do not need another reason to invest. They need to be shown which button to press.

Janice

Janice@janiice.myla

So you have got a spare 20 pounds lying around and you wanna know how to invest it, but you do not know how. Let me show you what I would do as a 22 year old grad with over 10,000 pounds invested into the stock market. Step one. You need an app or platform to invest on.

Watch on TikTok (167,333 views, 16,758 likes, 451 comments, 2,241 shares). A tap by tap guide to investing a first twenty pounds.

Her walkthrough stays inside the British tax wrapper that makes small starts efficient. Open a stocks and shares ISA first, she says, so gains up to the 20,000 pound annual allowance stay free of tax, and skip the specialist accounts until the basics are funded. For the investment itself she points at a broad index fund tracking the largest American companies, cites its long run record near 8 percent a year, and closes with the least glamorous instruction on the page. Fund it, buy, close the app, and come back only to add more.

Where they agree, and where they split

The consensus across all four is unusually total. Start now rather than after further study, begin with an amount small enough to survive contact with real bills, automate the transfer so willpower retires from the process, and buy broad low cost index funds instead of stock tips. Trell automates 250 dollars monthly, Melissa orders the 3 percent contribution plus the 25 dollar index purchase, Tatiana journals the target and invests through the year, Janice funds the ISA and closes the app. Four countries of temperament, one shared curriculum, which is rare enough on money social media to notice.

Let me show you why it is so important to save your first $10,000.

Trell the Trainer on TikTok

The split is about certainty, and it is real. Trell speaks in guarantees, projecting eleven million dollars and presenting compounding as a promise, while Melissa issues the same confidence as commands, complete with a multimillion dollar destination stated as fact. Janice answers from the other side of that confidence with the only caution on the page, and it deserves quoting in full.

And it is really important to know that your money can go up as well as down. So I would literally close the app, go do anything else, and only log back in when I want to put more money into it.

Janice on TikTok

Tatiana sits between the two camps without quite joining either. She sets bold public goals and celebrates loudly, yet her own numbers concede how much of the result came from markets rather than effort, 85,000 pounds saved against a 118,000 pound rise. The weight of voices favours starting immediately with small automated sums, and the only dissent worth keeping is Janice on volatility. Expect the line to wobble on the way up, and check the balance rarely enough that the wobble never becomes a decision.

Open the right account this week, a workplace plan to the full match or an IRA or ISA you fund yourself. Automate a sum small enough to keep, even twenty pounds or twenty five dollars, and invest the transfer the same day into a broad index fund so the cart is never empty. Pay down high cost card balances alongside, since interest works against every contribution. Then close the app, keep the job, raise contributions with each raise, and let time convert the unremarkable first deposit into the figure future ledgers will find hard to believe.
