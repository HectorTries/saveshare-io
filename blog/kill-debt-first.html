Kill High-Interest Debt Before You Invest: The Creators' Consensus

Credit cards, Klarna and student loans — what to clear first and why

← All money advice Debt

Investing while paying 25%+ APR on credit cards is like filling a bath with the plug out. The creators are unanimous: clear expensive debt first.

Credit cards and Klarna first

Mia Rose (@miarosemcgrath): "Get rid of high interest debt. I'm talking credit cards, Klarna."

— Mia Rose (TikTok source ↗)

Payday routine: debts second

Hannah Bevington (@hannahbevington): "The second thing I do [on payday] is I pay off any high interest debts." Systems beat willpower.

— Hannah Bevington (TikTok source ↗)

Sophie's net-worth inflection point

Sophie Blank (@itssophieblank), now at £230,000 net worth: "Once I paid [my student loan] off, my net worth skyrocketed." Guaranteed returns from debt payoff beat uncertain market returns.

— Sophie Blank (TikTok source ↗)

Sinking funds: never go into debt again

Sophie again: "My sinking funds are the one reason I pay off my credit card every single month and I never go into debt." Pots set aside ahead of known expenses mean the credit card stays at zero.

— Sophie Blank (TikTok source ↗)

Hannah's red line

Hannah counts "paying off a ton of credit cards" and "in a lot of debt" among reasons you probably shouldn't be investing at all yet. Harsh, but the maths backs her: no index fund reliably beats 24% APR.

— Hannah Bevington (TikTok source ↗)

The one rule

List debts by interest rate, attack the highest first, and automate minimums on the rest. Invest only once debts above ~7% are gone.
