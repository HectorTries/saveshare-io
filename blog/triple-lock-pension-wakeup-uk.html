Gabriel opens his video with four words that land like a door closing. Triple Lock is officially over. In the clip, watched 171,162 times with 7,126 likes and 2,059 comments, he is talking about an announcement that the guarantee on the state pension, rising each year by the highest of inflation, earnings or 2.5 percent, is set to be scrapped come 2030 and replaced with a rise linked to the higher of 2.5 percent or prices. Whether his viewers agree or not, and the comment count suggests many do not, the direction of travel is unmistakable. The state will do less, so the personal pension has to do more.

The tension running through every voice on this page is the gap between that shrinking promise and the balances people actually hold. Tommy Everitt has the numbers for the average 30 year old in Britain, and they are not the numbers of a generation ready to fund its own retirement. Ciara went from zero to £100,000 invested in just under five years, which sounds like a different universe until she lists the three unglamorous payday rules that got her there. Tatiana has four old workplace pensions and a system for taming them. DiDi is building wealth in her twenties without giving up travel and joy. The question none of them dodge is the one the triple lock news forces on everyone. If the state pension grows more slowly from here, what exactly is your plan, in pounds, this month.

Gabriel leads because his video is the news itself, the most watched clip on this page by a distance, and because he explains the mechanism in under a minute. In a follow up video he walks through what the Triple Lock even is, a guarantee launched in 2011, and why it costs what it costs, while asking his audience directly whether ending it is right or whether pensioners should keep getting more money.

Gabriel

Gabriel@gabriel.nussbaum

Pensions are about to change forever. And here is what you need to know. The Triple Lock was launched in 2011 and guarantees that the state pension rises by the highest of 2.5 percent average earnings or inflation. So why is it even being killed in the first place. Well, it is expensive. Like 154 billion pounds a year expensive.

Watch on TikTok (6,155 views, 234 likes, 67 comments, 5 shares). His explainer on what the guarantee is and why it costs so much.

Gabriel frames the cost as a transfer between generations, with younger workers funding a guarantee they may never enjoy themselves. His earlier triple lock video drew 171,162 views precisely because the politics feel personal either way. His position is blunt, the maths had to give, and the audience response shows how rarely blunt wins everyone over.

Tommy Everitt supplies the baseline that makes Gabriel's news sting. In a video watched 119,286 times with 1,507 likes, 106 comments and 423 shares, he lines up five numbers for the average 30 year old in the UK, and the pension figure sits in the middle like a quiet alarm.

Tommy Everitt

Tommy Everitt@tommytalks.uk

How well are you actually doing compared to the average 30 year old in the UK, let us find out. Number one is annual salary, and it sits at 40,668 pounds. Number two is total savings, and on average it sits at 11,023 pounds. Number three is the average pension balance held, and it currently sits at 10,660 pounds.

Watch on TikTok (119,286 views, 1,507 likes, 106 comments, 423 shares). Five averages at age 30, with the pension balance the one to beat.

The rest of his list completes the picture, £273 saved each month and 14.4 percent of earnings put away on average. Tommy never preaches, he just reads the averages. Against a state pension growing more slowly, £10,660 at 30 turns Gabriel's policy story into a personal one.

Ciara is what beating the average looks like from the inside. Her video, watched 33,418 times with 2,242 likes, opens with a result, zero to £100,000 invested in just under five years, then rewinds to the three payday rules that built it. The pension sits at rule three, after the buffer and the debt clearing, and her wording on the employer match is close to a command.

Ciara

Ciara@ciara__anne

Do these three things as soon as you get paid. I went from zero to 100,000 pounds invested in just under five years. And these are the three things I wish I knew. You need to be saving three to six months emergency fund. I know this is a lot of money and it does not sound very fun, but you need to do it.

Watch on TikTok (33,418 views, 2,242 likes). Her three payday rules, buffer first, high interest debt second, investing third.

Her second rule draws a hard line at high interest debt, credit cards and payday loans above 7 percent, while mortgages and student loans sit in a separate category. Her third rule is the one that matters most on this page. If the employer offers a workplace pension match, take it in full, because anything less leaves free money on the table, then keep going with a stocks and shares ISA. In a separate video with 1,541 views she notes only 16 percent of UK adults hold a stocks and shares ISA, arguing the wrapper deserves more love, partly because watching a named pot grow keeps savers going.

Tatiana answers the question Ciara raises but never fully unpacks, which is what to do about the trail of old workplace pensions most careers leave behind. Filming her pensions series for women she believes are underinvested, she starts with her own archaeology, four employers across 13 years, every pot located, then consolidated into one place for three reasons she counts on camera.

Tatiana

Tatiana@tatianamamixo

I have had four employers in my 13 year career span and I know where all of my pension investments are. Do you. The reason for this is that I have consolidated my pensions into one pot, and this has three main benefits to it. The first is that it simplifies my administration and gives me visibility of where my investments are.

Watch on TikTok (478 views, 60 likes, 4 comments, 4 shares). Her pensions consolidation walkthrough, fees, visibility and control.

The other two benefits are money in its purest form, potential fee reductions against older providers charging for nothing, and better investment control with wider fund choices and more flexible drawdown options. Her warning is careful, check the terms, fees and drawdown options before moving anything. Elsewhere she shows the funding engine behind it, £3,600 a month into her workplace pension by salary sacrifice, taken before she ever sees it, plus £1,400 into a self invested pension, part of a year in which she is tracking toward £100,000 saved and invested. Consolidation is the tidying, salary sacrifice is the engine.

DiDi closes the circle with the objection that stops most twenty somethings before they start, the fear that saving for 60 means starving 25. Filming in a video with 995 views, 133 likes and 7 comments, she names both appetites in the same breath, the love of living in the moment and the love of treating herself, set against the wish to plan for retirement and build long term wealth.

DiDi

DiDi@didifinance

You want to save money for your future, but you also want to live your best life in your 20s. You love living in the moment, but you also want to plan for retirement. You love treating yourself, but you also want to build long term wealth. Then you have come to the right place.

Watch on TikTok (995 views, 133 likes, 7 comments, 4 shares). Her intentional wealth pitch, travel now and compound later.

Her answer to the split is a word she repeats like a brand, intentional. She is tired, she says, of finance voices telling everyone to be frugal and spend nothing, because some people genuinely enjoy travel and experiences and new things. Her claim is that millionaires are separated from dreamers by one habit, action, and the job now is building wealth on purpose while still enjoying the decade. Where Gabriel counts the billions and Tommy reads the averages, DiDi supplies the permission slip.

Where they agree, and where they split

On the core claim there is no daylight anywhere on this page. Every creator treats the personal pension as the load bearing wall now that the state guarantee is wobbling. Gabriel reports the policy, Tommy prices the gap, Ciara and Tatiana show the funding machinery, DiDi keeps the saver in the game. Nobody argues the state will cover you. The unanimous vote is that the workplace pension comes first, the match gets taken in full, and the surplus keeps flowing after that.

The split opens on method and mood, and the fault lines are honest ones. Gabriel is the analyst, comfortable calling the triple lock expensive at £154 billion a year and cheering its end as generational fairness, a stance his 2,059 commenters clearly do not all share. Tommy is the measurer, content to lay out £10,660 against £11,023 and let the reader feel the shortfall. Ciara is the order of operations, buffer, then debt above 7 percent, then investing, with the pension match treated as non negotiable free money. Tatiana is the administrator, Consolidate first so fees stop leaking, then automate £5,000 a month across workplace and self invested pots. DiDi stands slightly apart from the mechanics, insisting that none of the plumbing matters if twenty somethings conclude retirement saving means cancelling their lives, so she sells action with joy attached. Gabriel would cut the promise to save the system, DiDi would sweeten the saving to save the saver, and both can be right at once because they are solving different ends of the same shrinking guarantee.

The deepest agreement hides inside the disagreement about urgency. Gabriel thinks the news itself should move people, the guarantee is going, act accordingly. Tommy thinks comparison should move them, here is the average, beat it. Ciara thinks process should move them, do these three things every payday. Tatiana thinks visibility should move them, gather the scattered pots and watch the fees fall. DiDi thinks identity should move them, become someone who builds wealth and books the trip. Five levers, one direction, toward the personal pot.

The practical close is unglamorous, which is the point. Find every old workplace pension and write down the balance, the fees and the match, consolidate only after checking the terms, take the full employer match this month, set salary sacrifice or an automatic transfer so the money leaves before you see it, and hold an ISA alongside for anything the pension locks away too tightly. Gabriel priced the policy at £154 billion a year. Tommy priced the average at £10,660. Ciara priced the method at three rules. DiDi priced the attitude at one word, intentional. The state promise is shrinking, and the five voices here agree on what fills the gap, your own automation, started this payday.
