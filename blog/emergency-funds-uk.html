Emergency Funds: How Much, Where to Keep It, and Sean's £75,000 Lesson

The safety net every creator mentions before investing a penny

← All money advice Saving

Before a single pound goes into the stock market, every serious creator says the same thing: build a safety net. Here's the advice, quoted.

3–6 months of expenses

Ollie Invests (@ollieinvests): "Before doing anything else, aim for three to six months of expenses put away in your emergency fund in a high yield savings account."

— Ollie Invests (TikTok source ↗)

Mia's 12-month buffer before quitting

Mia Rose (@miarosemcgrath): "Have 3 months worth of living expenses saved… when I left my 9 to 5, I had 12 months saved." Bigger life changes demand bigger buffers.

— Mia Rose (TikTok source ↗)

No emergency fund? Don't invest yet

Hannah Bevington (@hannahbevington) lists red flags for who shouldn't be investing — including anyone with no emergency pot to dip into, anyone raiding investments month-to-month to survive, and anyone carrying high-interest debt.

— Hannah Bevington (TikTok source ↗)

Sean's story: from zero savings to £75,000

Sean (@seans.money) had no savings after four years in work — then illness put him on statutory sick pay and he couldn't cover his mortgage. He went full frugal mode, built a three-month emergency fund, then kept going: five years later he holds around £75,000 in accessible savings plus ~£89,000 in pensions. "I will never allow myself to be in that position ever again."

— Sean (TikTok source ↗)

Where to keep it

High-yield instant-access savings — separate from spending money, earning interest, reachable within hours. Not invested, not locked away.

The one rule

Three months minimum, six if self-employed or single-income. Build it before you invest.
