Pension Basics: Free Employer Money, Salary Sacrifice and a £700K Projection

How UK creators think about pensions, from auto-enrolment to early retirement

The pension is the great paradox of British money: the most powerful wealth-building instrument available to ordinary workers, and the one they think about least. Free employer contributions. Full income-tax relief on the way in. Decades of tax-free compounding. And yet for most people under 35, the pension is a line on a payslip they have never clicked. The creators have opinions about this.

Ollie Invests frames it as free money with a small administrative catch. Walk through his five steps to take control of your finances and you arrive, at the end, at the pension: make the most of employer contributions, if you must contribute 5% for your employer to contribute 8%, that 8% is completely free money. Opting out of the match, in his telling, is declining a pay rise.

Neil Invests, 43, runs the sophisticated version. He contributes to his workplace pension via salary sacrifice, contributions leave his pay before tax and National Insurance are calculated, shrinking both bills legally. The result of decades of this discipline: a private pension projected around £700,000 by the time it kicks in at 57, sitting alongside £350,000 in accessible investment portfolios. His withdrawal plan runs at 5% across the pots, with the ISA bridging the years from his planned retirement at 45-50 until pension age. Two pots, two phases, one long game.

Neil's attitude to the state pension is worth quoting for its bluntness: he saves as if it will be pittiful-to-nothing by the time he reaches state pension age. Fend for yourself; provide for yourself. Whether or not you share his pessimism, the maths favours his posture, contributions made in your twenties compound for three decades, while contributions made in your fifties barely get started.

Sean shows the ordinary-earner version of the same story. Around £89,000 in pensions, most of it in an AJ Bell SIPP, built steadily since he joined the workforce in 2017, forming the largest slice of his roughly £165,000 invested. No six-figure salary required; just enrolment, patience, and never opting out.

Start with the employer match, contribute at least enough to capture every free pound. Route it through salary sacrifice if your employer offers it. Then leave it alone for thirty years, and let the least exciting account you own do the heaviest lifting in your financial life.
