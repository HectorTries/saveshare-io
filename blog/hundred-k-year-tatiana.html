The £100,000 Year: How Tatiana Is Saving Six Figures in 12 Months

£85,000 banked by September, a maxed-out ISA and £5,000 a month into the pension. Four more creators on the systems behind big-number years.

Tatiana has banked £85,000 toward a £100,000 saving year with a quarter still to run, and four more creators explain the systems behind big number years, from coast numbers to seven income streams.

Back to Blog Saving Investing Pensions UK

The scene is a September update delivered like a trophy lift with three months still unplayed. Tatiana, the London creator tracking a public mission to save and invest £100,000 in twelve months, opens the books on month nine to find the grand total at £85,000. The caption asks how to celebrate, and the clip drew 1,198 plays with 143 likes, 19 comments and 18 shares.

The mechanics matter more than the mood. The stocks and shares ISA is maxed out, so the full £5,000 saved that month flows into the pension, split between salary sacrifice at source and a self invested personal pension top up. Celebration planning looks less like tempting fate than simple arithmetic.

The question over the update is whether a £100,000 year comes from earning more or systemising more. Tatiana's numbers suggest income pushes while automation steers: money leaves before it can be spent, and willpower is politely removed from the process. The four voices below pull that idea in different directions, from patience plus compounding to income stacking at full tilt.

Monthly tracking is the engine of the project. Yo, we are gonna hit this goal." Publishing the running total each month turns a private hope into a public number, and public numbers are much harder to abandon.

September also shows where the machine points once the ISA allowance is gone. Salary sacrifice takes £3,600 before payday, £1,400 tops up the self invested personal pension, and the saver never meets the money she saves.

"I do this two ways. 1 by my pension salary sacrifice directly at source. When I get paid, I don't even see that money. £3,600 is going straight into my workplace pension. And the remaining £1,400 is going into my self invested pension plan, otherwise known as a SIPP."

If Tatiana supplies the pace, Ciara supplies the patience. The curator behind Curated Wealth used a review of The Psychology of Money to explain a climb from zero to more than £100,000 invested in just under five years. The video drew 1,142 plays with 109 likes, 55 comments and 4 shares.

Ciara

Ciara@ciara__anne

This book changed how I think about money more than anything else, and I went from zero to over £100,000 invested in just under five years.

Watch on TikTok (1,142 plays, 109 likes, 55 comments, 4 shares). The Psychology of Money review and five lessons.

The central lesson is compound growth plus humility, in her own words: "You don't need to be a millionaire to start investing. You just need to start small and consistent and then you let compounding do the heavy lifting in your portfolio." The tortoise strategy, stated without apology, and the £100,000 figure gives the fable a receipt.

Her sharpest idea is the coast number: the portfolio size at which contributions stop and compounding alone carries the saver to a comfortable retirement. A finish line with a number on it beats an endless treadmill of more.

"Knowing when to call it and say I'm done is a real skill. One of the things I've tried to do with this is work out my coast by number. This is the amount of money that once I have it, I will be able to stop investing and it will coast me through to a good retirement."

Steve

Steve@calltoleap

My name is Steve I'm a former public school teacher and I can teach this in an elementary school way.

Watch on TikTok (3,663 plays, 254 likes, 22 comments, 75 shares). The 60 second late starter tutorial.

Three steps: a high yield savings account, then a taxable brokerage plus an IRA at Vanguard, Schwab or Fidelity, filled with low cost dividend index funds through monthly recurring deposits. The maths is the hook, in his words: "Set up monthly recurring deposits and buy low cost index funds that pay a dividend. Because if you contribute $2,000 a month for the next 20 years you'll have around $1.5 million with average stock market returns." The same deposits also spin off about $16,000 a year in dividends along the way.

The 4 percent rule closes the loop, turning the portfolio into permission to spend around $60,000 a year without running dry.

"You can use the 4% rule and this will allow you to withdraw money from your account without ever running out of money which means you'll be able to withdraw around $60,000 a year."

Where they agree, and where they split

Strip away the accents and the agreement is striking. Every voice automates first: salary sacrifice at source, monthly recurring deposits, investing before spending. Every voice starts small and stays consistent, parking the money in index funds or pension wrappers rather than stock tips. The machinery is the message.

The split opens on enough. Becca and Tatiana maximise income and shovel the surplus, seven pay cheques or a £5,000 monthly run rate, with the target always one rung higher. Ciara pulls the other way with her coast number, arguing that knowing when to stop is a skill. Trell insists sequence beats speed, while Steve proves the same machinery works for starters in their 40s and 50s, quietly deflating every excuse on both sides.

Automate a fixed amount on payday into a pension or ISA of low cost index funds. Keep lifestyle growth slower than income growth and give the surplus a named job. Start with whatever the budget survives today, since two accounts and twenty years beat perfect timing that never arrives. Check the running total monthly, because a published number is a kept promise.
