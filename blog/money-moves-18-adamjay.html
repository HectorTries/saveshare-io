5 Money Moves at 18: Adam Jay's Goals Before 30

What a 24-year-old accountant wishes he'd started at 18

His follow-up: "Five financial goals to reach before 30, coming from a 24 year old accountant who wish he started these things at 18." How Adam would invest his first £100 at 18

"The most important 60 seconds you'll watch all day — how I'd invest my first £100 at 18, step by step." His prescription: a global index fund inside a Stocks & Shares ISA, automated monthly, and left alone for decades. Boring on purpose.

Why starting at 18 beats starting at 28

DiDi Finance (@didifinance) states the compounding law simply: "The earlier you start, the less money you have to invest every month." £100 a month from 18 can beat £300 a month from 30, thanks to an extra decade of growth. Time is the asset teenagers have most of.

Janice (@janiice.myla): "I left uni with £10,000 in my investment portfolio." Starting during university — with tiny amounts — meant graduating with a four-figure compounding head start instead of zero.

The house-price reality check

Adam grounds the ambition in property maths: "You would need to earn £54,000 a year to afford the average house in the UK." Knowing the target early lets 18-year-olds aim their careers and Lifetime ISA contributions accordingly — the 25% LISA bonus rewards exactly this kind of early planning.

— adm.jay (TikTok source ↗)

The five moves, spelled out

Piecing together Adam's videos, the five moves run roughly: open a Stocks & Shares ISA the week you turn 18; build a one-month emergency buffer in instant-access savings; start pension contributions from the very first payslip (auto-enrolment does this, but don't opt out); set up an automated monthly investment — even £25 — into a global index fund; and keep learning, one money video or book chapter a week. None requires wealth to begin. All reward starting early. The emergency buffer deserves emphasis: without it, the first surprise bill lands on a credit card at 25% APR, and the debt spiral undoes months of investing progress. Sophie Blank (@itssophieblank) credits sinking funds — mini pots for predictable expenses — as "the one reason I pay off my credit card every single month and I never go into debt." Systems beat willpower, especially at 18.

— Sophie Blank (TikTok source ↗)

What £25 a month from 18 becomes

Run the rough numbers: £25 a month (£300 a year) growing at 7% real returns from age 18 to 68 is worth around £150,000 in today's money — from £15,000 of total contributions. Double it to £50 and you're near £300,000. Nobody needs precision here; the point is directional. Every year you delay costs disproportionately because the lost years are the ones that compound longest. That is why Adam, DiDi and Janice all converge on the same advice despite different styles: the amount barely matters, the start date is everything.

The one rule

Open the ISA at 18, automate anything — even £10 — into a global index fund, and let decades do the rest.
