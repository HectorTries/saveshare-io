Lifetime ISA Explained: The 25% Bonus UK Creators Use for First Homes

How the Lifetime ISA's free government bonus fast-tracks a house deposit

How the Lifetime ISA's free government bonus fast-tracks a house deposit

Save £4,000 into a Lifetime ISA each tax year and the government adds £1,000, a 25% bonus just for saving toward your first home or retirement. Here's how UK money creators explain it, in their own words.

The deal is simple: contribute up to £4,000 a year (inside your £20,000 ISA allowance), and the state tops it up by a quarter. Max it out every year and that's £1,000 of free money annually, £33,000 in bonuses alone if you start at 18 and run it to 50. James (James), who runs three pensions and three ISAs across a ~£400,000 allocation, treats the LISA as one specialist wrapper among many: each account used for what it does best, with the LISA's job being the deposit.

Sean (Sean) holds about £3,500 in a Lifetime ISA alongside £72,000 in Stocks & Shares ISAs and £89,000 in pensions, roughly £165,000 invested in total. His message to beginners: The LISA is the same principle at deposit scale: small, regular contributions plus the 25% top-up compound into a deposit faster than a standard savings account ever could.

Like the ISA choice itself, it depends on your timeline. Buying within a year or two? A Cash LISA keeps the deposit safe. Five-plus years away? A Stocks & Shares LISA lets the deposit grow in the market on top of the bonus. James's approach, "low-cost, globally diversified funds, bought consistently every month and left alone", is exactly how a long-horizon LISA should run.

Gabriel Nussbaum (@gabriel.nussbaum) covers Budget rumours, housing schemes and pension reform for young viewers, including the Your First Home 2.5%-deposit scheme from both sides. His takeaway: policy changes constantly, but a LISA locks in today's 25% bonus whatever ministers announce next. Open one with £1 now to start the 12-month qualifying clock, even if you're not ready to save seriously yet.

Via Gabriel Nussbaum (TikTok source ↗)

The Help to Buy ISA is closed to new savers, so the LISA is the only deposit account that still pays a government bonus, and at 25% on up to £4,000 a year, it beats the old scheme's limits. Against a plain savings account paying 4-5%, the LISA wins by a mile for anyone eligible: a £4,000 contribution becomes £5,000 instantly, a return no savings rate can touch. The trade-off is the lock-in, plain savings let you withdraw anytime, while the LISA penalises non-qualifying withdrawals. So keep your emergency fund separate, and only put deposit money you are confident is heading toward a first home into the LISA.

Buying isn't the only path: money left in a LISA until age 60 can be withdrawn penalty-free, bonus included, making it a useful top-up alongside a pension, especially for the self-employed with no employer contributions. You can keep paying in until 50, so a late starter at 35 still has 15 years of £1,000 bonuses, £15,000 of free money, ahead of them.

If there's any chance you'll buy a first home in the UK, open a Lifetime ISA today. The £1 starter contribution costs nothing, starts the clock, and keeps the 25% bonus on the table.
