The Low Buy Year: How US Creators Spend Less and Keep the Fun

Low buy rules, money dates, micropayment savings and price tag codes, intentional spending with numbers, told with numbers

By SaveShare.io · 16 October 2026

On the first of January, Danny sat down to announce a New Year resolution that sounded almost like a punishment, a second consecutive year of deliberately not buying things. The previous twelve months of restraint had worked so well that the only logical move was to go bigger. The video announcing it was watched 2,631,900 times, which says something about the moment, because restraint used to be the thing nobody posted about, and now it gets millions of views.

The tension inside every story on this page is the same, and it is not really about deprivation. It is about whether spending less requires feeling less, whether a budget is a cage or a lens. Some of these creators treat every purchase as a small investigation. Some treat the calendar as the tool and automation as the relief. One of them turned an audience into a savings account. They disagree about the mechanism and agree about the premise, which is that money left unexamined has a way of leaving.

Danny is 22, financially responsible by self description, carrying a six figure net worth, and the architect of a low buy year, a concept that needs explaining because it is not a no buy year. In a clip watched more than 2.6 million times, Danny laid out the philosophy with the cheer of someone who has cracked something.

Danny

Danny@dimeswithdanny

My 2024 low buy year was extremely successful, so my New Year resolution is to do even bigger and better in 2025. A low buy year does not mean no fun, it just means smarter spending. Intentional shopping to me means prioritizing quality over quantity and having a list of things as you need so you do not end up impulse spending on things you want.

Watch on TikTok (2,631,900 views, 1,901 likes). Price tracking and alerts keep the plan on track.

The method is specific rather than ascetic. Danny tracks price histories, sets drop alerts, compares prices across stores, and keeps a running needs list so that wants cannot smuggle themselves in disguised as needs. Quality over quantity is the stated rule, which is the oldest shopping advice in the world wearing new clothes, but the discipline of the list is doing the real work.

Danny runs the whole operation through a stack of apps, and in a second video, watched 162,410 times with 11,501 likes, walked through each one like a tour guide in a very boring museum that happens to make you rich.

Danny@dimeswithdanny

Here are my favourite money apps as a financially responsible 22 year old with a 6 figure net worth. Starting off strong we have Vanguard, this is what I use to invest with, I have both my Roth IRA and my regular brokerage account here. We then have Marcus by Goldman Sachs which is my high yield savings account, in here I keep six months of expenses for emergencies and I also set up different funds for things that I am saving for like travel and gifts. A couple of years ago I used Excel to track all my finances but recently I switched over to Notion and I have been obsessed.

Watch on TikTok (162,410 views, 11,501 likes). The Notion budgeting template has been downloaded by over 5,000 people.

Six months of expenses in a high yield account, separate sinking funds for travel and gifts, a budgeting template in Notion that 5,000 strangers have downloaded. Every dollar is assigned a job and a address, and that is the whole philosophy in miniature.

Melissa Jean-Baptiste would recognise the system and rename it. Where Danny builds the apparatus out of apps, Melissa builds it out of appointments. Her prescription is three habits sustained for 90 days, delivered in a video that functions as a quarterly sermon.

Melissa Jean-Baptiste

Melissa Jean-Baptiste@millennialindebt

First money habit is you are going to go on one money date every single month. On the first of the month. On that money date, you are going to look at your actual numbers. We are not estimating over here. We are looking at exactly how much money we brought in for the month and how much money we spent for the month.

Watch on TikTok (1,213 views, 174 likes). Habits two and three are automations and a 25 to 50 dollar monthly side income.

The money date is the centrepiece, a monthly appointment where estimation is banned and only actuals are allowed, and the second habit removes willpower from the equation afterward through automation, one bill on autopay, one transfer to savings, one investment contribution. Melissa is 38 and on track to retire with 3.3 million dollars, which she deploys the way other people deploy credentials. Ninety days of that, she says, changes a life.

Miranda took the opposite route to the same destination, and hers is the strangest budgeting system on this page. After clearing more than 45,000 dollars of debt in 307 days through daily micropayments funded by video engagement and viewer donations, she pointed the same machinery at a 5,000 dollar emergency fund. In a video watched 71,570 times with 19,314 likes, she walked through the arithmetic of day 309.

Miranda

Miranda@miranda_andrews

I recently gave myself 365 days to pay down over 45,000 dollars worth of debt. And thanks to all of the amazing people on TikTok and all the support that I received, I was able to become debt free as of day 307 just a few days ago. And the decision to continue the journey for the remaining 56 days to establish an emergency fund was made. Each engagement will be totaled and that will be multiplied by an eighth of a cent. And that will be my contribution towards the savings goal.

Watch on TikTok (71,570 views, 19,314 likes, 3,843 comments). That day the audience generated 26,545 engagements plus 1,723 dollars in donations.

An eighth of a cent per interaction, counted daily, converted into savings deposits, topped up with donations from strangers. That particular day produced a deposit of 1,756 dollars and 40 cents toward the 5,000 dollar goal. It is budgeting by public subscription, and it works on the same principle as Melissa money date, which is that attention beats intention. Miranda simply outsourced the attention to tens of thousands of witnesses.

Becca Bergmann brings the philosophy down to the level of a single household argument, and it might be the most relatable budgeting video on this page. Her four year old is outgrowing a toddler bed. The family just upgraded to a king, so a perfectly good queen sits in the basement. Her husband wants to buy a new twin. Becca thinks that is, in her words, crazy. The video drew 29,005 views and 2,684 likes, presumably from everyone who has ever had this exact fight.

Becca Bergmann

Becca@beccanomics101

My husband and I are in a fight, and I need you to help me prove him wrong. Our daughter is four. She is growing out of her toddler bed, so we are trying to transition her into a big girl bed. I think that is crazy because we already have a queen size bed. What I think is crazy is going out and buying a full size bed or a twin size bed when we have a perfectly good queen size bed in our basement right now. I feel like I am being very budget conscious right now.

Watch on TikTok (29,005 views, 2,684 likes). She notes the child will grow into the bed.

The queen bed dispute is Danny low buy year compressed into one purchase, which is the point. Use what you own, buy only from the list, let the child grow into the thing. Becca household previously paid down roughly 320,000 dollars in two years, so the instinct to reuse the mattress is not thrift as aesthetic but thrift as architecture.

Noah completes the picture from the buying side. If Danny system governs what to buy and Melissa governs when to look, Noah governs how to buy, starting with the secret codes on discount store price tags. In a video watched 199,526 times with 14,904 likes, he decoded them.

Noah

Noah@businesswithnoah

TJ Maxx does not want you to know this secret. This place never has anything good, that is because you do not know the secret code on the price tags. If the code says one then the item was made specifically for TJ Maxx, it is not designer quality so check it carefully before buying. A two means that it came straight from an actual retailer or brand store, those are the real deals that you want to grab.

Watch on TikTok (199,526 views, 14,904 likes). Six means part of a set, seven means already marked down.

It is a small skill with an outsized moral, which is that even inside the act of spending, attention pays. The shopper who reads the tag buys the genuine surplus instead of the manufactured outlet line. Noah audience follows him, by his account, precisely to learn which is which. Informed spending is still spending, but it spends half as much for twice the value, and that gap is a budget line nobody has to feel.

Where they agree, and where they split

On the core claim there is no daylight. Every creator here treats unexamined spending as the leak and attention as the sealant. Danny keeps a needs list. Melissa bans estimation. Miranda posts daily balances. Becca prices the bed she already owns. Noah reads the tag. The unanimous vote is that money must be looked at, regularly, in actual numbers, whatever ritual produces the looking.

The split opens on who does the looking and how often. Danny and Melissa automate the virtue so willpower is not load bearing, apps and autopay carrying the plan between monthly check ins. Miranda externalises it entirely, converting an audience into an accountability engine at an eighth of a cent per interaction. Becca keeps it marital and improvisational, a fight about a mattress that is really a budget meeting. Noah pushes the attention to the point of sale itself. As Melissa puts it, with these three financial habits, you are not only building consistency for yourself, you are also setting yourself up for success in the future.

The weight of voices sits with the automators. Daily public accounting works, Miranda 45,000 dollars prove it, but it demands a kind of stamina most people cannot rent. The quieter systems, the list, the date, the autopay, the reused bed, ask less and last longer. Nobody on this page got richer by earning dramatically more in the period described. They got richer by leaking less, which is available to anyone with a list and a calendar.

The practical close is unglamorous, which is the point. Start a needs list and buy only from it for one month. Put one money date on the calendar, look at actuals, automate one transfer before you leave the table. Before any purchase over a threshold you set, check what you already own and read the tag. Danny is filming year two of buying less. Miranda has 56 days to close out a 5,000 dollar emergency fund. Becca four year old gets the queen. Different lives, identical method, attention, arithmetic, repeat.
