Check Monthly, Then Stop: 3 Creators on Tracking Net Worth Without Obsessing

Humphrey's monthly-then-yearly tracking rule, Tatiana's £400K transparency habit, and Danny's account-by-account net worth system at 22.

By SaveShare.io · 27 November 2026

Key numbers

  • £400,000: Tatiana's net worth at 33, built the traditional way with serious investing from 25.
  • 10–20%: Monthly swings Humphrey says invested net worth can show, and why checking too often hurts.

Net worth tracking, done right, is a feedback loop. Done wrong, it is a stress machine. Three creators on knowing your number without letting it run your life.

Back to Blog Net Worth US UK

Most money advice tells you to track your net worth and stops there, as if knowing the number is the whole job. Humphrey thinks the more interesting question is how often you should look at it, because the answer changes as you get richer. His video (26,050 views, 1,043 likes) owns the contradiction: check monthly when starting out, then deliberately check less as money grows. Early on the number barely moves unless you move it, so watching is motivating. Later the market moves it for you, and watching that is just anxiety with extra steps.

Humphrey

Humphrey@humphreytalks

The biggest financial tip, out of all the financial tips I know, would be for you to keep track of your net worth on a monthly basis if you're starting out, but if you're more advanced or financially set, I would only check it once a year.

Watch on TikTok (26,050 views, 1,043 likes, 40 comments, 61 shares). The monthly-then-yearly tracking rule.

His method is deliberately simple. Add up every asset in one column, subtract every liability, and the difference is the number. Early on, your inputs move that number fast, so saving and earning more shows up immediately. The flip comes when most of the number sits in the market, and then monthly check-ins mostly measure volatility you cannot control.

"Check it as often as your decisions are actually what's moving the net worth number. And as you get more financially secure, you can feel free to check it a little bit less."

Humphrey on TikTok

If Humphrey brings the system, Tatiana brings the reason to believe it works for someone like you. A self-described regular girl who built almost £400,000 by 33 with no financial help, she shares her numbers because transparency changed her own expectations. Her first job was on a Goldman Sachs trading floor at 19, and she credits that exposure plus eight years of serious investing from 25. The life, she insists, was not super sacrificial.

Tatiana

Tatiana@tatianamamixo

As a regular girl who has built almost £400,000 net worth by 33 and with no financial help, I want to talk about why I come on this internet and talk so openly about money.

Watch on TikTok (2,068 views, 268 likes, 13 comments, 11 shares). Why she shares her numbers publicly.

Her argument is that representation is a financial tool. Seeing someone who looks like you build wealth expands your sense of what is possible. She credits watching Mia McGrath turn expertise into income as what made her pick up her phone and start recording: the chain runs from watching, to believing, to doing.

"You just can't underestimate how much seeing someone else do it exposes you to the art of what's possible."

Tatiana on TikTok

Then the practical question: where do the assets actually live? Danny, 22, with a six-figure net worth, answers with a full tour of her accounts. Checking handles paychecks and bills only, never savings. A high-yield savings account holds four pots: a six-month emergency fund, travel, gifts, and a 25-percent taxes fund for her self-employed income. Then retirement accounts, a Roth IRA plus a 401(k) with the match if one exists, and a regular brokerage for money needed before 60. Her video drew 7,904 views and 34 comments.

The detail that lands hardest is her impatience with delay. Retirement feels distant at 22, which is exactly why she calls it the most important time to care, when every pound has decades to compound. It looks like a lot, she says, but it is not that confusing.

"So many people in their 20s disregard retirement because it seems so far away. But right now is literally the most important time to care about retirement because your money has more time to compound."

Danny on TikTok

Where they agree, and where they split

On the central question there is no real disagreement. All three treat the net worth number as something you should know and track, not fear or ignore. Humphrey gives the cadence, Tatiana gives the motivation, Danny gives the plumbing. The number tells you where you stand, other people's numbers show you where you could go, and the account structure is how you get there.

"Check it as often as your decisions are actually what's moving the net worth number."

Humphrey on TikTok

The split is about what the number is for. Humphrey treats it as a private dashboard, glanced at less as wealth grows. Tatiana treats it as a public good, shared to widen everyone else's ambitions. Danny treats it as an engineering output, the sum of accounts each doing one job. Track closely enough to steer, loosely enough to sleep, and openly enough to pull someone else up.

Run your own number this weekend. List every asset, subtract every debt, and revisit it monthly. Give each pot a job, borrow Tatiana's belief that the number can grow from anywhere, and follow Humphrey's cadence: monthly while your decisions move it, yearly once the market does.
