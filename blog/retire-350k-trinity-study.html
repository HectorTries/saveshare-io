Can You Retire on £350K? Neil's 4% Rule Walkthrough

The Trinity-study maths behind living off your investments

← All money advice Retirement

Neil Invests (@neilinvests) asks the question outright: "Can you retire on 350 grand? That's what I've got in my investment portfolios at the moment."

— Neil Invests (TikTok source ↗)

The 4% rule, UK edition

Neil explains: "£350,000 in an investment pot means that you can relinquish a certain amount… with 350k, if you can live on £14,000, which is 4% of that balance, then potentially you [can retire]." £14,000 a year — roughly £1,167 a month — from a £350K pot, adjusted for inflation, lasting decades through market ups and downs.

— Neil Invests (TikTok source ↗)

It depends on your circumstances

"350K might be enough for some people and it might not for others." Paid-off housing, low spending, and other income (partner, side hustle, later state pension) all shift the line. Someone spending £14K a year with no rent is in a different universe from someone spending £30K with a mortgage.

— Neil Invests (TikTok source ↗)

Neil's own plan: 5% plus a £700K pension

Neil, 43, takes a 5% withdrawal rate because he also has a private pension kicking in at 57, projected around £700,000. His strategy: retire around 45–50, live off the £350K ISA/investment bridge, then let the pension take over. Two pots, two phases.

— Neil Invests (TikTok source ↗)

The Trinity study in one paragraph

The "4% rule" comes from the Trinity study (1998): a 50/50 stock/bond portfolio survived 30-year retirements at 4% inflation-adjusted withdrawals in nearly all historical scenarios. Critics note today's valuations, fees and longer retirements — hence many UK planners now suggest 3–3.5% for extra safety, or Neil's approach of a higher early rate backstopped by a later pension.

Contributions matter most

Neil's other key line: "The biggest thing that's gonna impact your portfolio performance for a number of years is your contributions into it, not the returns." You can't control markets; you can control what you put in.

— Neil Invests (TikTok source ↗)

The one rule

Multiply your annual spending by 25 — that's your 4%-rule target. Then keep contributing until you get there.
