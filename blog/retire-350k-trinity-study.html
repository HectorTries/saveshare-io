The Trinity-study maths behind living off your investments

← Blog Retirement

At 37, Neil Invests decided he wanted to retire early. Not vaguely, not someday, but as a project with numbers attached, and he has been filming the maths ever since for an audience that keeps growing. His videos walk through the exact question this post tries to answer. How much is enough, how does the spending rate work, and what does the path look like from an ordinary salary. Around him, four other creators supply the missing pieces, the monthly amounts by age, the formula for starting at 40, and the living proof that boring consistency beats brilliance.

Neil frames the whole endeavour as a decision first and a calculation second. In a video watched more than 16,000 times, he explains that very few people reach financial independence by 50, then lays out the mindset that separates those who do, starting before confidence arrives and treating contributions as the engine.

Neil Invests

Neil Invests@neilinvests

At the age of 37, I decided that I wanted to retire early. Very few people manage to reach a level of financial independence by the time they are 50. You can, if you really want to get out years before everyone else. The biggest thing that is going to impact your portfolio for years is not your returns, it is your contributions into it.

Watch on TikTok (16,611 views, 294 likes). His most commented retirement video, with 38 comments debating timelines.

Neil describes holding around 350,000 pounds across investment portfolios, and his most shared clips return obsessively to two themes, the four percent rule that converts a pot into an income, and the unglamorous truth that monthly deposits matter more than market genius for the first decade. An 18 percent gain in two weeks, he tells viewers in a video seen 12,920 times, is a phenomenal snapshot but a terrible plan. He is building his financial future by putting 90 percent of his focus on contributions, and the snapshots can take care of themselves.

DiDi supplies the table that makes Neil's ambition concrete. In a video watched 13,162 times, she works the compound maths at seven percent annual returns and reads out the monthly price of a million pound retirement at every starting age, and the staircase she describes is the single most persuasive advert for starting young ever filmed on this site.

DiDi

DiDi@didifinance

How much do you need to invest every month to retire a millionaire? Let us do the maths. We are going to assume that you invest in the S and P 500, which returns an average of 7 percent every single year. At the age of 50, you would need to invest 3,100 pounds every month. At the age of 40, you would need to invest 1,250 pounds. At the age of 30, you would need to invest 535 pounds. At the age of 25, you would need to invest 350 pounds. Watch on TikTok (13,162 views, 1,014 likes). Her follow up shows 100 pounds a month for 30 years becoming 117,000 pounds, and 500 a month becoming 585,000.

The unfinished sentence is the point. At 18 the number is pocket money. At 50 it is a second rent. Every decade of delay roughly doubles the monthly cost, which is why Didi pairs this video with a compounding demonstration, 100 pounds a month for 30 years means 36,000 invested becoming 117,000, while 500 a month means 180,000 becoming 585,000. Past performance guarantees nothing, as her disclaimer notes, but the shape of the maths does not change. Time is the asset. Money is just how you buy it.

Dahlia speaks directly to the viewers Didi's table frightens, the forty somethings starting from zero. In a video walking through five steps for a 40 year old with nothing saved, she refuses the counsel of despair and prices the comeback precisely.

Dahlia

Dahlia@getontopwithdahlia

You have not started too late and you could still be a millionaire for retirement starting at age 40 if you start investing now and make it a regular habit. I want to give you an example. If you start investing 850 pounds per month from age 40 to age 65 into a stocks and shares ISA, into index funds such as the FTSE All World, you can still build life changing wealth.

Watch on TikTok (1,031 views). Her companion video lists five things she would do from scratch at 40, starting with opening a self invested personal pension.

Dahlia is a limited company director of sixteen years who posts tax tips and monthly habit systems, and her late starter material carries the authority of someone who manages real money rather than theorising about it. Her prescription for 40 is unglamorous and complete. Open the pension, max the ISA, buy the whole world through one index fund, automate everything, and let 25 years of compounding do the heavy lifting. Eight hundred and fifty pounds a month is serious money, and she never pretends otherwise. But it is a plan, with a number, which is more than despair ever offered.

Fellas Finance is the proof that the plan works when followed without drama. Eight years of monthly investing, every single month since 2017, a portfolio near 140,000 pounds, and a philosophy he summarises with a shrug that infuriates viewers hunting for secrets.

Fellas Finance

Fellas Finance@fellasfinance

After investing every month for the last eight years, and in that time building a portfolio of nearly 140,000 pounds, I have learnt that you do not need to waste time trying to pick winning stocks. Nor do you even need to figure out the perfect time to invest. Instead, you just need to get these three things right. Invest every month, keep your costs low, and buy the whole market.

Watch on TikTok (7,136 views). He also invests 1,500 pounds monthly, split between ISA and pension, as shown in a video with 25,300 views.

When commenters accuse him of repeating himself, he agrees with them, cheerfully, in a video with 3,073 views. They are right, he says. Building wealth is boring, and you have to do the same thing repeatedly for a very long time. People waiting for a secret are waiting for something that does not exist. The secret is the repetition.

Charlie brings the most aggressive timeline on the page. A 28 year old in Manchester on a 2,606 pound salary plus 1,050 pounds of rental income, budgeting publicly every month toward retiring by 40, with a six figure portfolio already built and early mistakes, thousands lost at the start, disclosed rather than hidden.

Where they agree, and where they split, is the question of how much urgency a viewer should feel. Neil and Didi form the early start caucus. Begin at 18 or 25, contribute modestly, and compounding carries you. Dahlia leads the late starter defence. Begin at 40, contribute heavily, and the maths still works. Fellas refuses the debate entirely, arguing that starting date matters less than never missing a month once begun. Charlie compresses the whole argument into twelve years of intensity. The consensus beneath the quarrel is absolute. Everyone invests monthly into whole market funds, everyone names a number and a date, and nobody, not one voice on this page, is waiting for the right moment to begin.

The takeaway is a single equation with three variables. Pick your retirement age, look up your monthly number, Didi's table gives the shape, 350 pounds at 25 or 1,250 at 40 for a million at seven percent, and automate it into a whole market index fund inside an ISA and pension before the month tempts you otherwise. Neil decided at 37 and holds 350,000 pounds. Fellas never missed a month in eight years and holds 140,000. The four percent rule then converts the pot to a wage. A 350,000 pound portfolio funds roughly 14,000 pounds a year. A million funds 40,000. Choose your number, fund it monthly, and let the decades argue among themselves.

Didi saves her most startling number for last. After pricing every decade from 50 down to 25, she reveals what the same million costs to someone starting at 18, and the figure is small enough to fit inside a teenager's part time wages.
