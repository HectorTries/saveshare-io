Can You Retire on £350K? Neil's 4% Rule Walkthrough

The Trinity-study maths behind living off your investments

Neil Invests opens with the question every saver eventually asks, usually at 2am: He is 43, holding £350,000 in accessible investments with a £700,000 pension projected behind it, and he has done the withdrawal maths in public, which is more than most financial advisers do behind closed doors. His walkthrough of the 4% rule is the clearest on the British internet. Fourteen thousand a year, roughly £1,167 a month, drawn inflation-adjusted from a £350K pot, lasting decades through crashes and recoveries alike. That is the promise, first quantified by the 1998 Trinity study, which found a 50/50 stock-bond portfolio survived thirty-year retirements at 4% withdrawals in nearly every historical scenario on record.

Neil is honest about the asterisk, too. "350K might be enough for some people and it might not for others." A mortgage-free spender at £14K a year lives in a different universe from a renter spending £30K, housing costs, partners, side income and the eventual state pension all move the line. The rule is a starting point for arithmetic, not a permission slip.

His own plan runs hotter than the textbooks. Because a private pension worth around £700,000 kicks in at 57, Neil withdraws at roughly 5% from the accessible pot, retiring around 45-50, living off the £350K ISA-and-investment bridge, then letting the pension take over. Two pots, two phases: spend the flexible money first, guard the locked money until it unlocks. Critics of the classic 4% rule, who note today's valuations, fees and longer retirements, and suggest 3-3.5% for safety, would find Neil's structure reassuring rather than reckless. The backstop is the strategy.

And underneath the withdrawal mechanics sits Neil's deeper sermon, the line he repeats across videos because it dismantles every excuse: You cannot control markets. You can control what you put in, which means the 4% rule is really a savings rule in disguise: multiply your annual spending by 25, and keep contributing until the pot matches.
