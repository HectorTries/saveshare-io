One $7M business, one $71K hustle year, one £68,000 spreadsheet

Back to Blog Income Side Hustle US UK

Tori Dunlap has 5.6 million followers and makes about seven to eight million dollars a year, which is exactly why her warning lands so hard. All these influencers are quitting to go back to corporate, she says, and she knows why. They blew up in 2020, gained tens of thousands of followers a day, quit their jobs on the strength of brand deals, and are now watching that income dry up as algorithms shift and content stops performing. One source of income they cannot control, and the moment brands stop paying, they are finished. Her video on the subject has been watched over 1.3 million times, which suggests the anxiety is widespread.

The tension here is between earning more and owning what you earn. A following is distribution you rent, a business is an asset you own. Different scales, same thesis. Revenue you control beats attention you borrow.

Tori Dunlap

Tori Dunlap@herfirst100k

When you are an influencer and just an influencer, your entire brand is built on brand deals, right? Your entire business is this source of income that you cannot control. So the moment that dries up, the moment brands decide to stop paying you, you are finished. What do you do if you are a smart, strategic CEO and business owner? You create a business that is well diversified so that if one source of income dries up, you are not completely finished.

Watch on TikTok (1,310,611 views, 80,736 likes, 2,081 comments, 3,000 shares). Why influencers are going back to corporate.

Tori blew up in the same 2020 wave as the people now retreating to corporate. The difference is she converted attention into infrastructure while they converted it into rent payments.

Erin Confortini shows what the diversified version looks like at the starting line. At 22, working a corporate job, she made $71,000 in one calendar year across freelancing, content creation and selling spreadsheets as digital products. After taxes and expenses that left around $46,000, aimed at $705 a month of student loan and car payments, gone within 18 months. Killing the payments cut her cost of living, buying her the solo apartment and, later, the option to go fully self employed.

Erin Confortini

Erin Confortini@itserinconfortini

That 71K was from a mix of freelancing, content creation, and selling digital products. Obviously, you have to factor in taxes and business expenses, so that left me with around $46,000. My goal for my side hustle in that first year was primarily just paying off my debt. I was already maxing out my Roth IRA every month, so my side hustle money was my opportunity to become debt free.

Charlie runs the same playbook from London with a spreadsheet and a controversial rule. At 28, Charlie has side hustled £68,000 this year in the pockets of dead time, evenings, lunches, even the bus to work. Every pound is tracked, and the controversial rule is that all of it gets invested. Funding lifestyle with hustle income just traps you into hustling forever.

Five money rules I live by as a financially responsible 28 year old who has side hustled £68,000 this year. The first one is work does not finish after my nine to five, my five to nine, lunches and even on the bus to work are those little pockets of time that I used to focus on my side hustles. Number three is probably the most controversial, but I invest all of my side hustling income.

The hustle is a bridge with a toll booth, not a treadmill. A £100,000 target for 2026, broken into monthly goals, funds digital products and systems before family plans change the maths.

Where they agree, and where they split

On the core claim there is no daylight. All three treat a single income source as a risk to be hedged, whether that source is brand deals, a salary, or one hustle. Tori diversifies a business, Erin stacked three hustle types from day one, Charlie tracks every pound and converts it into invested assets. The unanimous vote is that control beats size. A small income you own outperforms a large one you rent.

The split opens on what the surplus is for. Tori reinvests into the machine, team, products, infrastructure that outlives any algorithm. Erin aimed hers at debt, buying optionality by cutting fixed costs. Charlie invests all of it, refusing lifestyle inflation outright on the way to semi-retirement at 40. Same engine, three destinations, each chosen before the money arrived.

Nobody here got rich from one viral video. If there is one sentence all three would sign, it is Tori's. Build a business, not just a following.

The practical close is unglamorous, which is the point. List every income source and what happens if each vanishes tomorrow. Start a second stream this month, track it like Charlie, and give the money a job before it arrives like Erin. The algorithm will change. The bus to work will still be there.
