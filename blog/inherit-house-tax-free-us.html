Don't Hand Over the House: The $900,000 Tax Mistake One Creator Explains in 60 Seconds

John Liang's 161,000-view trust vs gift lesson, plus the HSA triple-tax play and the $7,500 custodial Roth move for kids.

The most expensive gifts are the ones that look free. A house handed over with love can arrive with a six-figure tax bill attached, and the paperwork decides which.

How you hold and pass money matters as much as how you earn it. Three US creators prove it: a trust erasing a $900,000 bill, an HSA invested like a Roth, and a custodial Roth making kids millionaires.

The headline act is John Liang, whose skit plays father and son discussing the family house: bought in 1980 for $100,000, now worth $1 million, and Dad wants to sign it over. The video drew 161,369 views with 18,539 likes, 84 comments and 1,599 shares, and the son's answer is blunt: do not sign it over.

John Liang

John Liang@johnsfinancetips

Do not let your parents pass you their house. Do this instead... If you pass it into my name and I turn around and sell it for $1 million, I would be on the hook for a massive tax bill on the capital gains of $900,000. Instead, if you set up a trust and put the house inside the trust and name me as a beneficiary, when you go 6 feet under, I will get the house at a stepped up basis.

Watch on TikTok (161,369 views, 18,539 likes, 84 comments, 1,599 shares). Trust vs gift: the stepped-up basis lesson.

The maths is brutal. A gift passes the original $100,000 cost basis to the son, so selling at $1 million leaves a $900,000 taxable gain. Inheriting the same house through a trust resets the basis to the $1 million present-day value, so selling at $1 million owes nothing. Same house, same sale, different paperwork: a six-figure bill or zero.

"Step up basis. It's just fancy rich people talk for saying that the cost resets to its present day value of $1 million."

John Liang on TikTok

From the house to the health account: Nate argues the HSA is the most underrated account in America. His explainer drew 3,141 views with 160 likes, 19 comments and 53 shares: most people leave the money in cash.

Nate

Nate@calltoleap

Most people don't know that the HSA is one of the most powerful accounts in the United States... You get tax free contributions, tax free growth, and tax free withdrawals for qualified medical expenses... Most people let their money sit in an HSA and do nothing. But in reality they can invest in this account just like a Roth IRA.

Watch on TikTok (3,141 views, 160 likes, 19 comments, 53 shares). The HSA triple tax advantage.

The 2026 HSA limits run $4,400 for self-only coverage and $8,750 for family, plus a $1,000 catch-up from age 55. Nate's play is to invest the balance like a Roth IRA, pay medical bills out of pocket, and keep digital receipts. With no time limit on reimbursements, the investments compound for decades before you reimburse yourself tax-free. Maxed and invested for 30 years, the account can top $1 million.

"Pay the bill out of pocket, keep the receipt, and let the invested HSA compound — then reimburse yourself tax-free years later."

Nate on TikTok

Finally, the generational play. Noah argues making your kids millionaires is easier than you think. His $7,500 custodial Roth video earned 1,352 views with 100 likes, 2 comments and 26 shares, and the strategy is deliberately boring.

Noah

Noah@businesswithnoah

If you want your kids to be millionaires, it's easier than you think... I know that most advisors can't outperform the market, and investing in your complicated strategies will charge me a ton in fees. I'd like to just buy some easy, boring index funds... And make sure to put them in his Roth IRA so he doesn't have to pay any taxes.

Watch on TikTok (1,352 views, 100 likes, 2 comments, 26 shares). The $7,500 custodial Roth move for kids.

Put $7,500 into a custodial Roth IRA and buy boring low-fee index funds, not an advisor's hand-picked stocks. The money grows tax-free for decades, which is what turns a small early deposit into a millionaire-maker. The catch is small but real: the kid needs earned income, and contributions are capped at the lesser of earnings or the annual limit.

"Boring index funds in a Roth the kid never touches: that is the whole strategy."

Noah on TikTok

Where they agree, and where they split

All three agree: the tax wrapper matters as much as the asset. The same house arrives with a $900,000 bill as a gift, clean through a trust. HSA cash left sitting earns nothing; invested, it compounds tax-free. A kid's deposit in a Roth grows untouched.

Where they split is direction. John is defensive: stop the bill before it lands. Nate is optimisation: squeeze the triple advantage from an account you own. Noah is generational: start the clock at birth.

Talk to an estate attorney before retitling any property. Check your HSA eligibility — you need a high-deductible plan — and invest the balance. If your kid has earned income, open a custodial Roth. Keep receipts for everything.
