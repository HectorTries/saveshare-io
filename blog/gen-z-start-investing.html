Gen-Z Starting Out: Holly, Janice and the 20 Pound Beginning

What the youngest UK money creators teach about starting from zero

Janice was 22, freshly graduated, holding more than 10,000 pounds in investments, and filming the tutorial she wished someone had made for her at 18. It has now been watched 167,000 times by people mostly younger than she is, which makes it one of the most consequential videos on this site. The premise is disarmingly small. You have a spare 20 pounds. Here is exactly what to do with it. No jargon, no minimums, no waiting until you earn real money, because the central claim of every creator on this page is that waiting is the only real mistake.

Janice@janiice.myla

So you have got a spare 20 pounds lying around and you want to know how to invest it, but you do not know how. Let me show you what I would do as a 22 year old grad with over 10,000 pounds invested into the stock market. Step one, you need an app or platform to invest on. I use Trading 212 because I find it really easy to understand. It is free and it has no minimum deposit.

Watch on TikTok (167,333 views, 16,758 likes). The most watched beginner investing video in the SaveShare database.

Janice built the 10,000 pounds the way she teaches, 100 pounds a month automated from a part time job while still at university, plus every spare pound swept in at month end. In her graduate retrospective, watched nearly 35,000 times, she stresses that the starting amount is almost comically flexible, 2 pounds, a fiver, a tenner, literally whatever you have, because the habit compounds while the amount scales with earnings. This is the answer to every teenager who says investing is for rich people. A graduate with a part time job automated her way to five figures before collecting her degree.

Janice@janiice.myla

I left uni with 10,000 pounds in my investment portfolio. I would automate an amount that I would put into my trading account every month. I chose 100 pounds because I had a part time job at the time. But you could literally do it with any amount. You could start with 2 pounds, a fiver, a tenner, it literally depends on you.

Watch on TikTok (34,930 views, 4,444 likes). Her what I would do again guide for students starting today.

Mia Rose and her sister Emily turn the same lesson into a live experiment. Mia is 25, Emily is 21, and in a video watched nearly 40,000 times they model identical 100 pound monthly investments at seven percent, starting four years apart. By 60, the younger sister holds 53,000 pounds more, not from brilliance but from birth order. Mia built her own multiple six figure net worth with 100,000 invested the same way, and her beginner guide, seen 66,796 times, insists on the preconditions young investors skip, three months of expenses saved, consumer debt cleared, and only long term money invested.

Mia Rose@miarosemcgrath

This is my sister Emily. She is 21 years old. I am 25 years old. Let us just say we both start investing 100 pounds a month. By age 60, Emily would end up with 53,000 pounds more than me just because she started four years earlier. I think that goes to show the power of compound interest and starting while you are young.

Watch on TikTok (39,859 views, 1,682 likes). Filmed as a sister versus sister experiment with viewers running their own numbers.

Holly is the youngest voice on this page and the most honest about the learning curve. Her trading diary videos document small wins and open positions without polish, a 595 pound day trading trend lines that left her buzzing, smaller gold trades still running, strategies tested tomorrow and reported either way. The sums are modest and the transparency is total, which is exactly the combination missing from most finfluencer content aimed at teenagers.

595 pounds, that is how much we made today. 595 pounds trading with trend lines, because it is the strategy that I have been testing. I am currently in a smaller trade as well for gold. I am actually absolutely buzzing, so I just wanted to tell you guys about it. I am just pretty proud of myself just documenting.

Ollie Invests brings the institutional memory. His premium bond explainers and stock drop breakdowns, like his Nike analysis when the shares fell ten percent in a week and fifty percent across the year, teach young viewers to distinguish price from value. And Sean documents the destination, a 65,000 pound stocks and shares ISA paying real dividends, built month by month, the living proof that Janice starters become Sean holders if they simply continue.

Sean@seansmoney

Here is the July dividend update for my Trading 212 stocks and shares ISA with 65,000 pounds invested. The dividends land, the contributions go in, and the balance does what balances do when you stop interfering with it.

Watch on TikTok (Documented monthly). His ISA dividend series tracks a real 65,000 pound portfolio payment by payment.

Where they agree, and where they split, is the question of what young beginners should buy first, and the answers form a deliberate ladder. Janice says a whole market fund through a free app, Holly says a tested strategy with money you can afford to lose, Ollie says understand single stocks before touching them, Mia says global funds inside an ISA after the buffer exists, Sean says whatever you buy, leave it alone and let dividends arrive. Nobody says wait. Nobody says you need thousands. The only argument is about the vehicle, and every vehicle named beats the savings account, which is the comparison that actually matters at 18.

The takeaway is Janice starter kit, priced for a teenager. Download a free platform with no minimum, automate whatever you can, 20 pounds, 100 pounds, a fiver, into a whole market fund inside a stocks and shares ISA, sweep the month end surplus, and do not check the balance except to admire the dividends. Mia sister holds 53,000 pounds extra from starting at 21 instead of 25. Janice held 10,000 at graduation. Sean holds 65,000 and counting. The ladder is fully documented, every rung filmed, and the first rung costs less than a takeaway.

Janice student loan video adds the balance every young investor needs. Freshly graduated, she films exactly what she owes, then shows the portfolio she built alongside the debt, 10,000 pounds invested while studying, proof that student borrowing and market investing can coexist when the debt is low interest and the investing is automated. The video draws thousands of views from students who assumed debt meant waiting, and Janice answer is characteristically precise. Expensive debt first, student debt in parallel, markets throughout.

Janice@janiice.myla

I graduated in July and this is how much I owe in student loan. Plan 5 terms, nine percent over the threshold, written off after forty years. I built 10,000 pounds of investments while studying anyway, because the loan works like a graduate tax and the market works like compounding. Do both, in that order of priority.

Watch on TikTok (2,432 views, 140 likes). Her student loan versus investing explainer for graduates doing the real maths.

The distinction rescues a generation from paralysis. Credit cards at 20 percent must die before investing begins, as every creator here agrees. Student loans at nine percent over a high threshold, written off in forty years, are a different animal, and Janice built five figures treating them that way. Young viewers who conflate all debt stay out of markets for a decade. Janice stayed in from 18, and the 10,000 pounds at graduation is the receipt.

Janice still automates from her graduate salary, Mia still films the sister maths, Holly still documents the small trades, Ollie still explains the price drops, and Sean still collects the dividends. Five creators, five ages, one ladder. Twenty pounds becomes a hundred a month, becomes ten thousand at graduation, becomes sixty five thousand paying dividends. The first rung costs less than a takeaway. The last rung pays more than a salary. Start climbing.

The student loan confusion keeps thousands out of markets they could already be in. Expensive borrowing must die first, every creator here agrees, but graduate debt on Plan 5 terms behaves like a tax while compounding behaves like a raise. Janice held both at once and graduated with ten thousand invested. Mia sister holds fifty three thousand extra from starting at twenty one. The market does not ask your age. It asks only when you arrived, and earlier is always cheaper.
